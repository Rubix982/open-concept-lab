Went out for Asr today, and on the way back I took a detour.

There is a park on the way home, and I ended up walking in it for a while. No plan, nothing to finish, not even thinking about anything in particular. Just walking, and letting the day come off me.

It was beautiful in a way I do not usually stop for. Everything at that hour is its own colour, nothing trying to be anything else, and the light comes in sideways and makes ordinary things look considered. Grass, a wall, somebody's back as they walk ahead of you. I was not looking for any of it. It was just there, the way it is there every evening whether or not I come out to see it.

Then I looked at the sun going down, and something odd happened, the way it always does. Years came back at once. Things that hurt, things I have grieved, things that made me happy, things I am still hoping for -- all of it in one look, not in order, not as memories exactly. More like the whole of it passing in front of me in a second. A flash of life. I do not know why the setting sun does this and almost nothing else does.

And standing there I had a thought that I want to write down before it goes, because it felt true in a way my thoughts usually are not:

There is no reason to feel tired or unhappy.

I am young. I can still work on myself. I can still turn things around. Whatever I have made a mess of so far is not finished, and whatever I have not started yet is still available to start. That is not optimism, it is just the arithmetic of where I am.

And there are people much older than me still going through it -- still carrying things, still trying, still getting up in the morning to face something they did not choose. If they can do that with the time they have left, what business do I have meeting my own life with less courage? What exactly would be my excuse?

And it runs the other way too. If I do not meet life with courage, it gets heavier. Not because anything in it changed, but because deciding nothing is still a decision, and the weight of it keeps collecting while I am not choosing. Courage is not the brave part. It is just the part where I stop letting the thing sit.

So whether I do something or do not do it has to come from somewhere settled -- from some honest view of what life is actually like -- and not from whatever I happen to be carrying that day.

Because what I carry changes what I see, and it does it quietly. Something unresolved inside does not announce itself. It just turns the world down a little. It is like wearing a glass that makes you see less: vision confused, fog over everything, and the thing standing directly in front of you is the one thing you cannot make out. You are not blind, you are just not being shown it.

And then -- this is the part that gets me -- it takes the language too. You cannot go over what is buried inside and say it properly, because the words for it are behind the same fog. So the thing stays buried, and stays heavy, and keeps dimming the glass. That is the whole loop, and it runs on its own.

Which is why I think the decision has to come first, before anything else in the day. Before the work, before the list. Let things unwind as they will. Do not let stress narrow the world down to less than what is actually there, because what is actually there is usually more than enough, and most of what I ever needed was standing in front of me while I was looking through the wrong glass.

---

I notice I only ever reach this on days when I was not trying to. Nothing about this evening was planned. I went to pray, I came back a different way, and in the gap between those two things something settled that weeks of sitting with it had not settled.

It is the same shape as the other thing I keep learning -- that the useful thought shows up when I stop arranging the conditions for it. I do not fully understand why. Maybe the mind needs the hands to be busy with something unimportant before it will let go of what it is holding. Or maybe it is simpler than that, and I just need to be somewhere other than at a desk.

Allah has arranged it so that the evening arrives whether or not I have finished anything. That is a kindness I do not think about enough. The day closes on its own schedule, the light goes whether the work went well or badly, and tomorrow starts again from where it starts. There is a mercy in a thing that does not wait for me to be ready.

Anyway. I walked a bit more, and came home.

... And life felt very beautiful, which I am writing down mostly so that I will believe it later, on a day when it does not.
