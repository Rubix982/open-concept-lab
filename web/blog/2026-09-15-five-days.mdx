This is the record, not the argument. The argument is
[the previous post](/writing/the-check-that-was-never-there); this is everything
around it, including the parts that failed.

It is written to be as useful where a claim was wrong as where it held, because
most of them were wrong.

## Where it started, where it is

The project charter, written on day one:

> ~50 CounterFact edits, GPT2-medium, ROME. Two hand-built probe sets per edit:
> consequences (forward) and grounds (backward). Measure contradiction rate in
> each. Deliverable is a two-panel figure and a number.

Five days later: **zero edits have been applied.** No two-panel figure, no
contradiction rate. The project is named `edit-slice` and has not yet edited
anything.

What exists instead is a delivered tool, a published claim, and four models
measured — which came out of building infrastructure for the thing that didn't
happen. Four of the charter's premises turned out to be false, and each was
falsified by measurement rather than argument.

## One claim's full life

Claim 1 is the best illustration of the whole pattern, because it went through
every state a claim can occupy. Following it end to end is more useful than the
ledger above.

**Day 1 — confident.** *Backward probing is unexplored. Everything in the
literature probes forward: edit a fact, check its consequences. Nobody probes the
grounds that made the original fact hold.*

**Day 2 — suspicious.** The claim was too comfortable, which is usually a bad
sign. The obvious place for it to break was RippleEdits <Cite id="cohen2024ripple" />,
whose *Logical Generalization* criterion covers symmetric and inverse relations.
Symmetric relations sound backward. If that is already a grounds probe, the claim
isn't narrow — it's taken.

Reading the definitions rather than a summary turned up two things.

A bookkeeping one first: a note in another repo described that paper as EMNLP
2023. It is TACL 2024. Secondhand notes decay silently, and I had been treating
mine as though they could not.

Then the definition itself. Logical Generalization says relations satisfy logical
constraints — Sibling is symmetric, so if `(e, Sibling, o)` holds then
`(o, Sibling, e)` does too — and checks whether those survive the edit. So it
*does* probe something that looks backward: edit *(Eiffel, located-in, Rome)* and
it asks what Rome contains.

But that is the **same triple with its arguments swapped** — the edit restated, not
the reasons for it. The transitive case is sharper still: to get from "Eiffel is in
Rome" to "Eiffel is in Italy" you must *use* the fact that Rome is in Italy, and
the benchmark only ever checks the conclusion. Whether the premise survived is
never asked.

That distinction became load-bearing enough to write into the project's binding
definitions:

<Figure plain caption="The distinction the narrowing produced. Everything downstream depends on it.">

| | what it means | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Argument order** | swap the arguments of the edited fact | covered by RippleEdits since 2024 |
| **Justification order** | the *distinct* facts whose truth was a premise for the edited fact | not covered by anything we can find |

</Figure>

"The Eiffel Tower was built for the 1889 Paris Exposition" is not the location fact
inverted. It is a different fact about a different thing, and it is the one that
goes stale in silence.

**Day 2, continued — narrowed.** Not *backward probing is unexplored*, but:
*existing ripple evaluation probes the logical closure of the edited triple;
inverse and symmetric probes exist and are backward in argument order; nothing
probes backward in justification order.* Less exciting, more defensible, and it
cost an afternoon. Better to find the counterexample to your own claim than have a
reviewer find it in a room, in front of people.

**Day 3 — withdrawn entirely.** A second literature pass found CounterFact
apparently checking possession already, so the broader framing was given up and a
weaker one written in its place.

**Day 5 — restored, and verified.** Reading the ROME paper's construction appendix
<Cite id="meng2022rome" /> rather than its prose showed the withdrawal was the
error. Records are built *"solely using the PARAREL entry"*, with no model
consulted at any point.

Three things that generalise, none of them profound, all of them relearned the
hard way:

1. **Read the primary source before designing around a summary.** Every error in
   this sequence — the wrong venue, the almost-scooped claim, the false withdrawal
   — came from trusting a note or a search result over the paper.
2. **Go looking for the strongest counterexample to your own idea, early.** If it
   holds you have lost a week. If it doesn't you have gained a defensible sentence.
3. **A claim getting smaller is progress** — and a claim getting smaller *wrongly*
   is recoverable, provided the reason for each move was written down.

## Routes taken and abandoned

Six routes were opened and closed. Each is recorded with what killed it, because a
route that dies for a stated reason is reusable knowledge and one that just goes
quiet is not.

### 1 · Mined logical rules over DBpedia — dead in 45 minutes

The plan: read probe questions off a knowledge graph instead of hand-writing them.
A published benchmark had already mined Horn rules from DBpedia with AMIE, so the
artifact existed. Read the rules backwards and the body of a rule whose head is the
edited fact *is* its justification set.

What the rules actually contained:

```
?a birthPlace ?h   ?b isPrimaryTopicOf ?h   =>   ?a birthPlace ?b
?a hometown   ?g   ?g commonName       ?b   =>   ?a hometown   ?b
```

Read it: *if a was born in ?h, and ?b is the same entity under another name, then a
was born in ?b*. That is preservation of a fact under **renaming the object** — a
tautology about DBpedia's redundant naming predicates.

Of 12,675 mined rules, median support was **1**. Of the 913 that survived a
support/confidence filter, **66.5% were alias tautologies**, 21.2% were headed by an
alias predicate, and **2.3% were substantive** — and those headed on `gdpPppYear`
and `topLevelDomain`.

All 32 rules with a head matching one of our target relations were alias
tautologies. Not thin: zero.

<Aside>
The deeper reason only became clear four days later. Rule mining finds{" "}
<strong>chain</strong> patterns, and what we were asking for are{" "}
<strong>star</strong> relations — see below. No amount of KG quality would have
fixed it; we were asking a chain-finder for a structure it does not represent.
</Aside>

### 2 · AGM kernel contraction — dropped after five cases

The best idea of the week, and it lasted a day. Reframing forward/backward as
**expansion/contraction** imports fifty years of belief-revision results instead of
restating an observation: editors only ever *add*, contraction is where
non-determinism lives, and Hansson's kernels give a formal definition of the
justification set.

Then we read five actual cases:

| post-edit state | contradictory? |
| --- | --- |
| died in Barcelona, buried in Baltimore | No — bodies are repatriated |
| developer IBM, publisher Sega | No — these differ routinely |
| named after Hamburg, located in London | No — things are named after distant places |
| native language Russian, born in Paris | No — families emigrate |

**None of them.** Kernels are minimal *entailing* subsets; if nothing entails,
there is nothing to break and contraction is undefined.

What survived was AGM's *other* half — the ordering machinery (entrenchment, Grove
spheres, Lewis closest-world) rather than the closure machinery. We kept the
account of the problem and dropped the algorithm.

And the loss strengthened the thesis. If grounds entailed conclusions, a machine
could compute what to retract and human discretion would be a convenience. Because
they don't, **someone has to choose** — which is the project's actual argument.

### 3 · The containment chain family — dead in a day, and our own table said so

To get a real contradiction we needed grounds that genuinely entail. Transitive
containment looked right:

```
X located-in Y   &   Y located-in Z   ⊨   X located-in Z
```

Edit the outer to some other country and the two inners still entail the original.
62 chains mined from 120 seeds.

Then: `probes/relation_modality.md`, our own artifact from three days earlier,
labels `P131` **mutable** — *"boundaries are redrawn."* So editing the outer admits
a temporal escape (*"it is in Italy now"*) — the exact reconciliation that made the
kernel cases non-contradictory.

We had written the table for precisely this purpose and did not consult it.

### 4 · The diagnostic framing — withdrawn, then restored

Claimed: *editing benchmarks measure propagation into knowledge they never verified
was there.* A literature pass found CounterFact appearing to handle it, so the
claim was withdrawn and a weaker one written up.

Reading the paper's **construction appendix** rather than its prose reversed that:
records are built *"solely using the PARAREL entry"*, with the counterfactual drawn
by weighted sampling over the same predicate and **no model consulted at any
point**. The benchmark's own table then confirms it — unedited GPT-2-XL scores
**ES = 22.2**, meaning 22% of records already favour the *false* target.

Two literature passes missed this because both searched for *competing work*
instead of re-reading the *primary source*. The paper was in a local directory the
whole time.

### 5 · Pairing possession with a structure map — split, not merged

The tempting move: ship possession *and* a map of the belief neighbourhood around a
fact. Rejected. Possession was complete and sharp; the structure map was neither,
and its value was as foundation for an arc that was blocked. Pairing a finished
thing with an unfinished one makes both worse.

### 6 · Grounds by directed intervention — right method, wrong order

Define *g is a ground of f* iff intervening on `g` moves `f` **more than**
intervening on `f` moves `g` — causal and directed, discovered rather than
asserted, which escapes the circularity of hand-written probes.

Still the right method. It was overtaken because a ground the model does not hold
cannot be tested for direction, so possession had to come first. Parked, resumable.

## Four structural findings

These came from sitting with the premise rather than from running anything. Each
reorganised the project more than any measurement did.

### The graph is a star, not a chain

For four days we said "the graph gives us grounds." It does not. Look at what our
grounds had actually been:

> Perec's native language is French.
> Grounds found: born in Paris · citizen of France · speaks French.

Every one is **another property of the same subject**. Wikidata asserts *no edge*
between them. They are siblings on one node, and the justificational relation among
them is entirely our imposition.

Consequences, all recorded: rule mining failed because it finds chain patterns; and
`k`-hop depth — which the whole design was built around — **does not apply to the
star at all**, because a star has no path structure. The question "how far does it
propagate backward?" was malformed.

### Editors expand; they never contract

Forward propagation is **expansion**; backward is **contraction**. Every editing
method — ROME, MEMIT, fine-tuning — is an expansion operator: it pushes probability
toward a target. None has a contraction primitive.

Independent mechanistic support arrived from a paper we found while checking
something else: a compact mask over ROME's edited weights **restores the pre-edit
answer in over 70% of cases**. If the edit had overwritten anything, there would be
nothing left to restore. Edits *suppress*.

Which makes the orphan worse than modelled: the model ends up holding the new
answer, the old answer, **and** the grounds for the old answer, simultaneously.

### In-degree and out-degree are different variables

Out-degree — how many consequences a fact has — is the forward propagation burden,
and it is already studied. **In-degree** — how many independent justifications —
is the contraction burden, and it is not. Median in-degree in our data is **8**
co-predicates per subject; 1% of subjects have none; 84% have three or more.

### The decision nobody makes

The thesis, arrived at sideways. If logic cannot say which belief to give up, then
an editor that silently picks one is making an **unaccountable epistemic choice** —
at scale, invisibly, with nobody able to inspect or contest it.

So the orphan is not merely a technical defect. It is **a decision that got made
without anyone making it**. And an instrument's job is to surface the choice, never
to make it: *show the structure, never the ranking.*

result.

### The legs are not independent, and that is the useful number

The decision to move to 70B was written down the day before with a prediction
attached: *"possession is 56% on GPT-J against 79% at 70B, so the usable set should
roughly double."* It went up **3.7×**. The estimate treated a per-fact rate as if it
carried straight over to a per-chain one, and a chain is a conjunction — its rate
moves faster than any leg in it.

Having been wrong that way, the honest next question is what the conjunction
actually does. Multiplying the three marginals predicts 4.5% usable at GPT-J. The
measured figure is 14.7% — **3.27× what independence gives**. At 70B the same
comparison is 43.6% predicted against 54.4% measured, a lift of only **1.25**.

| pair | GPT-J lift over independence | 70B lift |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `inner_1` & `outer` — share the subject X | **2.10** | 1.15 |
| `inner_2` & `outer` | 1.53 | 1.09 |
| `inner_1` & `inner_2` — share no subject | 1.26 | 1.00 |

The lift is ordered by shared subject and flattens with scale. The obvious story is
that possession clusters per entity — a chain about a well-known person holds at
every leg, one about an obscure person at none — so the binding variable would be
subject familiarity rather than leg difficulty.

That story is a claim about a variable nobody had measured, so I measured it.
Prominence, taken as the subject's statement count in the Wikidata snapshot already
on disk, covers 136 of 136 chains and spans 5 to 404. Stratifying by it leaves the
lift **completely untouched**: 2.08 within-stratum against 2.10 pooled at GPT-J,
1.15 against 1.15 at 70B. A common cause would have collapsed the within-stratum
figure toward 1. It did not move at all.

Prominence is a real variable — it predicts the possession *level* at 70B, where
`inner_1` runs 78% to 93% and `outer` 46% to 68% from the low to the high tercile.
It is simply not the one binding the legs. And at 70B the lift is ~1.1 whichever way
the set is cut, which is close enough to independence that my framing of it as a
finding was overstated. What remains unexplained is a residual 1.61 at GPT-J.

This is the number worth carrying forward, because chain attrition is the cost model
for every longer chain anyone might build. Under independence a five-hop chain at
70B would be hopeless. Under the dependence we measured it is merely expensive.
Costing a multi-hop design off marginals alone will under-build it — which is the
same mistake as the prediction at the top of this section, one level up.

### The test that came back Deny, and was more useful for it

The surface-form hypothesis was worth one run, so it got a ticket with its outcomes
written down in advance: *confirm* if rendering countries naturally recovered the
article-taking names, *deny* if it did not. The same 136 items, the same model, the
same 28-country pool, one thing changed — how every candidate is spelled.

Held rate went from 19% to **16%**. On the headline number, Deny.

The rank data underneath says the opposite, and the pair is the actual result.

| condition | answer class | n | ranks 1st **with subject** | ranks 1st **with placeholder** | held |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `bare` | article-taking | 58 | 21% | 2% | 19% |
| `bare` | bare-name | 78 | 85% | 0% | 85% |
| `natural` | article-taking | 58 | **69%** | **69%** | **16%** |
| `natural` | bare-name | 78 | 83% | 0% | 83% |

Surface form was real: spelling it `" the United States"` moves rank-1 from 21% to
69%. The model was never failing to know where these people were born. It was
failing to complete an ungrammatical string.

And fixing that destroys the control. Under the natural spelling the *placeholder*
prompt ranks the same answer first at exactly the same rate — 69%. Of the 40 items
that now rank first with the real subject, **31 are rejected by the lift test, every
one of them because the placeholder ranked it first too.** *"[X] was born in the
country of the United States"* is the modal completion whether or not `[X]` means
anything at all.

So the two criteria have no common operating point. A spelling that makes the answer
rankable makes it prior-favoured; a spelling that suppresses the prior does it by
being ungrammatical. **There is no spelling that satisfies both** — which means our
possession filter cannot certify possession of a fact whose answer is simply the most
common answer for its relation. Bare-name countries sit at 0% placeholder rank-1
under both spellings, so the measure is sound there and *undefined* here.

That is a limit on the tool this project ships, not a fact about Llama, and it is the
most useful thing found all day. It also retracts the 102/136 ceiling from two
sections ago: under the natural spelling, usable chains go 74 → **71**.

The fix is a better control rather than a better spelling. Asking *"does the subject
matter at all"* is defeated by a modal answer by construction. The question worth
asking is *"does the model track **which** subject"* — score the answer against a
paired real subject whose true answer differs, and require the model to prefer the
right one. That discriminates knowledge from prior without needing the prior to be
low. It is opened as E-012, and deliberately not retrofitted into any number already
published here.

### Both fixes, and an interaction nobody predicted

E-012 built that control: for each item, take the score of its true answer under
every *foil* prompt — a real subject whose answer differs — and ask what fraction it
beats. That is an AUROC. Chance is 0.5. It is indifferent to how high the answer
scores overall, which is exactly why it is defined where the placeholder test is not.

Run alone, it gained **one chain**. Modal answers discriminated well — median AUC
0.901 — but the *row* test now blocked them, because `" United States"` still would
not rank first while it was still spelled ungrammatically. Thirty-two items sat on
that intersection: control satisfied, ranking not.

So the last run was the full 2×2.

where every modal answer lives.

`outer` and `inner_2` are dense at 100%.

| `logit[t] − logsumexp` instead of `log_softmax` | The latter allocates a second `[batch, tokens, 128k-vocab]` tensor. That, not batch size, was the actual OOM — and it was 2.5× worse on Llama than GPT-J purely from vocab size. |
| Transport errors classified by **module**, not type name | `httpx.ConnectTimeout` matched none of my hardcoded names and killed a 165-item run at item 28. |
| Dated, checksummed snapshots | Wikidata mutates. A result is not reproducible without the bytes that produced it. Snapshots are additive — one directory per ingestion, never overwritten. |
| Per-item caching everywhere | Three separate debugging cycles cost minutes instead of restarts. This is worth more than any of the fixes above. |

</Figure>

## The pattern in the errors

Eight reversals in five days. Sorting them by *where* the error sat produces the
only generalisation in this post worth carrying:

**None of the errors were in the measurements.** Every number held. What failed
each time was the **framing supplied around the number** — the mechanism I invented
for a phenomenon that had been measured correctly.

- The possession curve held; the story about *why* CounterFact's filter was weak
  was wrong twice, in opposite directions.
- Grounds were correctly found to be evidential; the claim that this made
  containment a contradiction was wrong.
- The alias tautologies were correctly counted; the theory that better data would
  fix them was wrong.

And a second, sharper pattern: **each error was already refuted by something we had
written down.**

## Six analogies, and where each one breaks

These are for explaining, not for claiming. An analogy that isn't bounded gets
used past its evidence, so each comes with the place it stops working.

### A drug trial that never checks the patients were sick

You give a cholesterol drug to a hundred people and measure whether cholesterol
drops. It doesn't drop in forty of them. But forty of them had normal cholesterol
to begin with, and nobody checked. *"The drug failed in 40%"* is now a statement
about recruitment, not about the drug.

The edit is the drug, the fact is the condition, and **CounterFact never screened
for the condition** — records are built from a knowledge graph with no model
consulted at any point.

<Aside>
Where it breaks: a drug trial has a control arm. Editing evaluation does not, and
cannot easily get one — there is no "unedited but otherwise identical" model to
compare against except the model itself, which is what the prior-arm control in the
possession measure is standing in for.
</Aside>

### "Does this student know the material?"

| exam | score |
| --- | ---: |
| Multiple choice, one obviously-wrong distractor | **75%** |
| Multiple choice, fifty plausible options | **61%** |
| Open-ended essay, ambiguously worded question | **12%** |

Same student, same knowledge, three defensible exams. None is cheating. The third
is not ignorance — it is the student answering a different question than the
examiner meant, which is exactly what *"Karolos Koun died at"* → `" the age of 90"`
is.

So any single possession number is **a choice about the exam, reported as a fact
about the student**.

### Circumstantial evidence, not a proof

*"He was buried in Baltimore"* is **evidence** that he died there. It is not proof;
bodies get shipped. So when you edit "died in Barcelona", the burial record does not
become *false* — it becomes *odd*. Nothing is contradicted. The story simply stops
hanging together.

Against that: *"born in Paris"* **and** *"Paris is in France"* entails *"born in
France"*. That is a proof. Change the conclusion and something has to give.

Almost everything surrounding a fact is circumstantial, which is why the chains had
to be hand-built — to manufacture the rare case where the evidence really is proof.

### The graph is a filing cabinet, not a citation trail

Someone's birth certificate, passport and employment record all sit in the same
folder. The folder records that they concern the same person. It does **not** record
that the passport depended on the birth certificate. That dependency lives in the
clerk's head, not in the filing system.

We spent four days saying "the graph gives us grounds". It gives us **the folder**.
The dependencies were always ours.

<Aside>
Where it breaks: real archives sometimes <em>do</em> record provenance — that is
what a chain of custody is. Wikidata mostly does not, and the properties that look
like provenance (<code>described by source</code>, <code>different from</code>) are
bookkeeping rather than justification.
</Aside>

### A redirect, not a deletion

The file is still on the server; a forwarding rule was added in front of it. Remove
the rule and the original serves again.

That one is close to literal. A compact mask over ROME's edited weights restores
the pre-edit answer in **over 70%** of cases — if anything had been overwritten
there would be nothing left to restore.

So after an edit the model holds the new answer, the old answer, **and** the
grounds for the old answer, simultaneously.

### The 92% that was really 52%

Armour the bombers where the *returning* planes were hit, and you armour exactly
the wrong places — the planes hit elsewhere never came back to be measured.

The possession filter's first run reported **92%** and had scored 12 of 40 edits.
The 28 that dropped out were the hard ones. The same survivorship error, inside the
tool built to detect it.

### And the one that covers all of them: constellations

The stars are really there. Position, brightness, distance — measurable, checkable,
and everyone who looks sees the same ones.

**Orion is not there.** The lines are drawn by us, and different cultures drew
different figures from identical stars.

That is the finding. A model's facts are real and measurable — 56%, 74%, 79%, 85%,
reproducible across runs. But every relation we tried to measure *between* them —
grounds, dependency, entailment, justification — turned out to be a line we were
drawing rather than a line we were finding. We kept pointing the telescope harder
at the gaps between the stars.

The useful part is that this does not make constellations worthless. Sailors
navigated by Orion for millennia. It means only that you have to say *"here is the
figure I am using, and here is why"* rather than *"here is the figure that is up
there."*

Which is the whole argument for human discretion. If the lines were really in the
sky, a machine could trace them. Because we draw them, **someone has to choose** —
and that someone should be a person who can be asked to justify the choice.

## Where the evidence points

Three statements, in increasing order of how much they cost to learn.

**The knowledge is thinner than everyone assumes.** Models hold 56–85% of a
*curated* benchmark's facts, not ~100%, and nothing in the standard pipeline checks.

**"Knowing" is not one property.** Three defensible measures gave 75%, 61%, and 12%
on identical items. That is not measurement error — they measure different things
and all three are legitimate. Any single possession number is a **choice being
reported as a fact**.

**And the structure we kept looking for was not there.** We wanted a dependency
graph: the KG asserts no edge between a fact and its grounds. We wanted entailment:
support is evidential and every "contradiction" had a satisfying world. We wanted
kernels: no minimal entailing sets exist. We wanted a decidable oracle: the mined
rules were naming tautologies.

A model's dispositions are genuinely there and genuinely measurable. But every
relation we tried to measure *between* those dispositions — grounds, dependency,

something we found.

That is not deflating, because it changes the kind of claim available. Not *"here
is the dependency graph inside GPT-J"* but *"here is the audit standard I am
holding it to, and here is why."* The first is a discovery that keeps failing to
replicate. The second is a position that can be argued with.

And it is why the discretion argument holds. If grounds entailed conclusions, a
machine could compute what to retract. Because they don't, **someone has to
decide** — which is the project's thesis and its longest-running embarrassment,
turning out to be the same observation.

## The edit finally ran, and it said no

Everything above was a gate on one experiment. It has now run.

Seventy-one chains survived the cleaning, forty-two survived a week of NDIF outages, and
each got two rank-one edits at layer 5 — ROME as configured by EasyEdit, which is to say
with `mom2_adjustment: false`, the unwhitened update every one of its fourteen shipped
configs uses.

Edit the conclusion — *"Vlaminck was born in the country of"* → Germany — and then ask
where he was born. The result is unambiguous and, for a while, thrilling:

| `inner_1` lands in the target country | rate |
| --- | ---: |
| baseline | 5% |
| **after the real edit** | **67%** |
| after a same-subject control edit (his occupation) | 5% |
| placebo country (the null) | 0% |

Edinburgh becomes Hamburg. Paris becomes Santiago. Tokyo becomes London. The model does
not merely lose the old answer — the control loses it too — it moves the answer somewhere
*consistent with the new conclusion*, and the control does not. Thirteen times the control
rate, placebo at the floor.

It reads exactly like the thing this project was built to find: the model gives up the
premise it can afford to give up — *which city* — and keeps the one it cannot — *that city
is in that country*. Defeasible yields, necessary survives. Belief revision, measured.

### The control that killed it

That reading has a cheaper rival, and the rival predicts the same data. The edit makes
inputs keyed on the subject emit a Germany-valued vector. The birth-city prompt shares the
subject, so its key is *similar*, so it receives a dose of the same vector. A
Germany-flavoured residual stream promotes Germany-associated tokens, and German cities are
Germany-associated. No inference anywhere — just content bleeding through a similar key.

The occupation control cannot separate these. It varies *content*, so it shows content
matters; it cannot show whether the content licenses an inference.

The control that can holds subject and target country **fixed** and varies only the
relation:

| edit | licenses *"born in a German city"*? |
| --- | --- |
| `X was born in the country of` → Germany | **yes**, deductively |
| `X works in the country of` → Germany | **no** |

Both push the same country-valued vector through the same subject's key. Only one licenses
the implication. Nobody infers a birthplace from an employer's country.

| arm | lands in target country |
| --- | ---: |
| birth-country edit | 28/42 = **67%** |
| work-country edit | 28/42 = **67%** |
| **paired difference** | **+0.0 pp** |

Exact McNemar on the discordant pairs: **p = 1.000**, nine each way. Capital share among
hits identical at 82%. Where both arms land in the target country, they choose the *same
city* 84% of the time.

Editing where a person **works** relocates their **birthplace** exactly as often as editing
where they were born.

### The city that ate a third of the results

One number in that run is worth more than the headline. Across the birth arm, **Washington,
D.C. absorbs 14 of 42 destinations — 33%.** In the work arm, 11 of 42.

It is the highest-prior city in our candidate pool, and it is where probability goes when no
coherent relocation happens. Which makes it the third appearance of one pattern: the US
being 40 of 136 countries, article-taking names losing to bare ones, and now a single city
acting as a sink. Concentrated pools with one dominant attractor have shaped three separate
results in this project, each time arriving disguised as a finding about the model.

Any pool we report from here reports its attractor mass alongside it, the way out-degree has
to report its edge vocabulary.

### What survives

The measurement stands; only its meaning shrank. Rank-one editing at this configuration
produces country-specific displacement into same-subject probes at 67% against a 5% control
— that is real, and it is a fact about what these editors do to knowledge they were not
aimed at, which is the charter of this project.

What does not survive is the inference. The model is not reasoning about what its new belief
implies. And the honest version of the orphan story gets worse rather than better: the edit
does not retract the grounds, and it does not coherently rewrite them either. It smears
country-flavoured probability over anything that mentions the subject.

## Whitening could not have changed the answer, and the reason is one line of algebra

The last post left a gap open and called it "the obvious place a genuine inference effect
could still hide": everything had been run with `C = I`, the unwhitened update EasyEdit
ships for all fourteen of its ROME configs.

It is not hiding there. It cannot be.

Start with what ROME's update actually does to an input it was not aimed at. The edit is
built from three things: the **key** read at the subject's last token of the edit prompt,
the **edit vector** the layer will add to its output there, and a **direction** along which
the weights are moved. Only two of them matter here — call the key `k*` and the direction
`u`. The whitening choice is entirely a choice of `u`: EasyEdit's default is `u = k*`, and
the whitened version is `u = C⁻¹k*`, where `C` is the second moment of the layer's keys.

For any other input with key `k`, the edited layer's output shifts by the edit vector
scaled by a single number:

    coefficient = (k · u) / (u · k*)

That is the whole mechanism of leakage. The edit reaches an unrelated input exactly as far
as that coefficient is large.

Now take a probe that shares the edit prompt's prefix up to the subject's last token —
*"Jack Marshall was born in the **city** of"* against the edited *"…in the **country** of"*.
Under causal attention the key at that position cannot see the later tokens, so it is the
same key: `k = k*`. Substitute:

    coefficient = (k* · u) / (u · k*) = 1

for **any** `u`. The normalisation divides by exactly the quantity it is about to produce.

  Whitening can only reduce leakage to prompts that do <em>not</em> share the subject's

The measurement agrees, and agrees boringly: forty-two chains, three values of λ, and the
birth arm's top-1 destination is identical to the unwhitened run in **42 of 42** every
time. The profile along one probe shows why — coefficient **1.0000** at the subject's last
token under both editors, and only after that do the two diverge (0.90× at the next token,
0.28× by the preposition).

### The cosine said it was a no-op, and the cosine was wrong

The gate that authorised this experiment nearly closed it with the right answer for the
wrong reason. Whitening barely rotates the update: `cos(k*, C⁻¹k*) = 0.97`. Read at speed,
that says the whitened editor is the same editor and there is nothing to test.

It is not the same editor. On held-out keys the mean absolute coefficient drops from 0.0260
to 0.0013 — **a twentyfold narrowing of reach** — at a cosine of 0.97. A near-identical
direction with a radically different blast radius is not a contradiction; the coefficient
depends on where `u` points *relative to the rest of the key distribution*, which is
precisely what a 0.97 cosine does not measure.

Had the gate stopped at the cosine, [E-016] would have closed with "whitening does nothing"
— true of the outcome, false about the editor, and useless as a reason. That is the second
time in this project the aggregate statistic and the component statistic disagreed and the
component was right.

### The escape hatch, tested and closed

The analytic account had one hole, and it was stated when the account was: it requires the
probe to share the edit prompt's prefix, which our probes do **by construction**. A probe
mentioning the subject later — *"The city where Jack Marshall was born is"* — shares no
prefix, and its coefficient is not pinned.

Moving the subject later was predicted to break the pinning. It attenuates it by seven
percent.

| probe form | mean coefficient | median | min |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| early — the edit form | **1.000** | 1.000 | 0.998 |
| **a different relation** — *died in the city of* | **1.000** | 1.000 | 0.998 |
| late clause — *the city where X was born is* | 0.935 | 0.954 | 0.670 |
| possessive — *the birthplace of X is* | 0.934 | 0.966 | 0.483 |
| long preamble | 0.925 | 0.946 | 0.707 |

The second row is the one that matters, and it is a case the analytic account did not
construct. A probe asking about a **different relation entirely** scores exactly 1.000
against a birth edit's key, because the subject sits at the start and causal attention makes
the key identical. The relation being asked about is irrelevant to how much of the edit
arrives.

So the layer-5 key at a subject's last token is largely determined by the subject tokens
themselves rather than by what surrounds them, and no natural reformulation escapes.

**Same-subject leakage in ROME is structural — with a boundary I did not have when I first
wrote that sentence.** Two claims were bundled in it, and a layer sweep separated them.

The **analytic** one holds everywhere. A probe sharing the edit prompt's prefix — including
one asking about an entirely *different relation* — scores exactly 1.000 at every layer
tested, from 0 to 31, minimum equal to maximum. That is not an empirical regularity; it is
`(k*·u)/(u·k*)`, and it cannot come out otherwise.

The **empirical** one is an early-layer fact. Probes that reformulate rather than share the
prefix — a late clause, a possessive, a long preamble — decay steadily with depth:

| | layer 0 | layer 5 | layer 12 | layer 24 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| late clause | 0.986 | 0.935 | 0.795 | 0.524 |
| possessive | 0.990 | 0.934 | 0.794 | 0.564 |
| long preamble | 0.979 | 0.924 | 0.764 | 0.444 |

So "regardless of where the subject sits, or how much preamble precedes it" is true at
layer 5 — the layer EasyEdit's config edits — and false by layer 24, where a reformulated
probe receives roughly half the edit vector. The subject key is largely determined by the
subject tokens early on, and stops being so with depth.

Layer 31 reads 0.699, which looks like a recovery and is not one. The different-subject
floor there is 0.669 — everything scores about the same whoever is named, so the
measurement has lost its ability to discriminate rather than the key having changed
character. Carrying that floor at every layer is the only reason the difference between
layer 24 and layer 31 is visible at all.

That sentence had a hole in it when I first published it, and it is worth saying how it got
closed. Every control behind the 93–100% figure varies the **form** and holds the subject
fixed. None of them varied the *subject*. So "any prompt containing the subject" was not
actually separated from "any prompt" — the floor was assumed, never measured, and
[E-016]'s own anisotropy number was a reason to doubt it: these keys sit on an effectively
26-dimensional manifold out of 2048, where arbitrary vectors have substantial overlap by
construction.

Measured now. A **different person** in the identical template — *"Marie Curie was born in
the city of"* scored against a Jack Marshall edit's key — comes back at **0.082**, against
0.958 for the same-subject forms. The distributions do not touch: the highest
different-subject coefficient is 0.153, the lowest same-subject one 0.483.

So the pinning is keyed on the subject, and now demonstrably so. The anisotropy worry was
a good reason to run the control and a bad prediction of what it would say.

This also finishes the account of the result that closed the last post. The work-country
edit and the birth-city probe both begin with the subject, so the probe receives the work
edit's full, unattenuated delta. **The +0.0 pp gap was not a null needing explanation — it
was structurally required.** No choice of `C` could have returned anything else.

### Two mechanisms I guessed, and both were wrong

Before measuring the coefficient profile I proposed two accounts of why whitening changed
nothing, and wrote both down. Neither survived.

The first was that the probe's key is aligned with `k*`, so whitening preserves it along
with everything else. Measured: `cos = 0.034`. They are near-orthogonal, and the alignment
story was not close.

The second was that the destination is scale-invariant — that the edit's magnitude does not
matter, only its direction, so shrinking coefficients would not move the answer. Measured:
a uniform ×0.27 rescale breaks relocation in three of four chains. Scale matters.

The third account was not a guess. It came out of the per-position profile, which was the
one thing neither guess had looked at. An earlier "3.7× attenuation" figure I had been
carrying turned out to be measured at the probe's **final** token, which contributes 0.03 of
a 1.66 total — two percent of the mass. It described a negligible part of the update
accurately.

### Honest limits

**The relocation half of the late-probe test is unrun, not negative.** The late form holds
the true city at baseline in 8 of 42 chains against 29 for the early form, which is below
the floor of 10 set before the run. Reporting it as a weak result would have read as
confirmation of the hypothesis under test, when the cause is that the probe does not work.

**Some subjects do attenuate materially.** The minima in that table — 0.483, 0.670, 0.707 —
are real. This is an existence-and-tendency claim about prompt forms, never a rate.

**Sixteen chains for the coefficient sweep, forty-two for the edit arm.** One model, one
layer, one relation family.

**The different-subject floor was measured with one pairing.** Subjects were paired by
rotation. A pairing chosen to be adversarial — matched on token length, or nationality —
could score higher, and was not run. The claim is that *these* different subjects score
0.082; that different subjects generally do is an inference from a clean separation, not a
measurement of the space.

**The sweep ran, and it narrowed the claim rather than confirming it.** The form-robustness
half is early-layer only; see the table above. What survives at all depths is the
prefix-sharing case, which is algebra rather than measurement.

**Delivery, never effect.** Every number here is how much of the edit vector arrives, not
whether it changes an answer. A ×0.27 rescale broke relocation in three of four chains, so
a fall from 0.93 to 0.52 is likely to matter behaviourally — but that is an inference, and
no edit was run at any layer but 5.

**Eight layers is a shape, not a per-layer claim**, and why the decay happens is unmeasured.
That the subject key becomes context-sensitive with depth describes the measurement; it
does not explain it.

**Do not read this as a proposal.** That reducing same-subject leakage requires changing the
normalisation or the key selection rather than the covariance is a consequence of the
finding, not a method I am offering. Any approach that limits an edit's blast radius by
reweighting `u` inherits this limit.

## Why the null was inevitable

A null result asks for an explanation, and the obvious suspect was the editor. Everything
above used `C = I` — the unwhitened update, which drops roughly eight nats on facts about
the subject that have nothing to do with the edit. A blunt instrument smears; maybe a
sharper one would show the inference the blunt one destroyed.

ROME's method section is built on the sharper version: `u = C⁻¹k*`, where `C` is the
second-moment matrix of the layer's keys. EasyEdit ships fourteen ROME configs and **every
one of them sets `mom2_adjustment: false`**, including the two models the ROME paper
itself used. So the whitening term the method is named for is off by default in the
toolkit most people run.

Turning it on is not free — `C` is 14336² at this model, 0.82 GB, and inverting it is
worse. But the key distribution turns out to live on a **26-dimensional manifold**: half
the variance in 16 directions, 99% in 338. So the honest model is low-rank plus a ridge,
and Woodbury computes `C⁻¹k*` while forming nothing bigger than an N×N matrix. Seventeen
megabytes instead of eight hundred.

### The statistic that nearly ended the investigation

The first check was the cosine between `k*` and `C⁻¹k*`. It came back **0.97** — whitening
barely rotates the update. On that number the whole line of inquiry closes: whitening
changes nothing, move on.

It was the wrong statistic. ROME changes an arbitrary input by
`(v* − Wk*) · (u·k)/(u·k*)`, so what whitening does is shrink that **coefficient** on
unrelated inputs — and a 0.97 cosine is perfectly compatible with a twentyfold change in
it. Measured on held-out keys: 0.0260 unwhitened against 0.0013 whitened. The update's
*direction* barely moves; its *reach* changes by a factor of twenty.

(The first version of that measurement evaluated on the same keys that defined `C`, which
gave a spectacular and meaningless 1000×. Refit on 1400, evaluated on 665 held-out. The
same denominator mistake as day two, in a new costume.)

So: a demonstrably twentyfold more selective editor. Run it on the same 42 chains.

**Forty-two out of forty-two identical destinations.** Paired gap +0.0 pp at every λ.

### The coefficient is pinned, and it is pinned by arithmetic

Three guesses at why, all wrong, all cheap to have tested first. That the probe key was
aligned with `k*` — it is nearly orthogonal, cosine 0.034. That the destination was
scale-invariant — rescaling by 0.27 breaks relocation in three chains of four. Only the
third attempt measured instead of predicting, and printed the coefficient at every
position of the probe:

| position | token | coeff `C = I` | coeff `C⁻¹` |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 7 | `'amin'` | 0.0918 | 0.0786 |
| **8** | **`'ck'`** | **1.0000** | **1.0000** |
| 9 | `' was'` | 0.1156 | 0.1040 |
| 14 | `' of'` | 0.0305 | 0.0086 |

Exactly one. Not approximately.

`k*` is read at the subject's last token of the edit prompt. A probe that begins with the
same subject has an **identical key** at that position — causal attention guarantees it,
since nothing differs in the tokens before. ROME divides by `u·k*`. So the coefficient
there is `(k*·u)/(u·k*) = 1` for **any** `u`, hence for any covariance anyone might choose.

That single fact explains every result in the arc:

| observation | why |
| --- | --- |
| the birth-city probe displaced, 67% | shares the prefix → pinned at 1.0 → full delta |
| *"Paris is located in…"* inert, −0.02 | shares no prefix → nothing pinned |
| whitening changed nothing, 42/42 | cannot touch a coefficient fixed by arithmetic |
| a uniform ×0.27 rescale **did** break it | it scales the pinned term too |

And it explains the work-country result that started this. The work edit and the birth
probe both begin with the subject. The probe receives the work edit's full, unattenuated
vector. The gap was not a null waiting to be explained — it was **structurally required**
to be zero.

### How far the claim actually goes

The tempting next sentence is "so same-subject leakage is unavoidable." Testing it across
depth says otherwise — and the test matters, because layer 5 is simply what EasyEdit's
config happens to edit.

What this does not establish: whether the decayed coefficient at layer 20 actually produces
less displacement. The scale test already showed coefficient and destination are not
linearly related, so it cannot be assumed. And the obvious follow-on — edit deeper, leak
less — is method design, which this project deliberately does not do.

## What is open

**The experiment the project is named for has run, and returned a negative.** Forty-two
chains, edited at layer 5, measured against a same-subject control and a relation-matched
control. Rank-one editing moves the birth-city answer into the edited country 67% of the
time against a 5% control — and does so identically when the edited relation licenses
nothing. Displacement, not revision.

**The mechanism is settled; its consequence for other people's benchmarks is not.** ROME's
`u·k*` normalisation pins the update coefficient to exactly 1 at the subject's last token,
so any prompt beginning with the edited subject receives the full vector — at every layer,
for any covariance. Every ripple and propagation benchmark we know of probes with prompts
that begin with the subject. What that does to their measurements has not been worked out
here, and it is the question this arc most obviously raises.

**The strongest remaining claim is about measurement, not editing.** A Known/Unknown
knowledge axis inherits surface form competition (Holtzman et al., EMNLP 2021), and we
measured the same facts changing classification on spelling alone — rank-1 on modal
answers moved 21% to 69%. Recent work conditions edit-success conclusions on exactly such
an axis. Whether those conclusions survive a phrasing-stable classification is open, and
it is the one question here that is both unclaimed and consequential.

**Nothing has been externally judged.** Every critique here is self-critique, which
has a ceiling — it catches what you already half-know, which is precisely the
pattern documented above. The five-day record is a large, internally consistent
body of work that nobody outside has stress-tested. That is the real risk, and no
amount of further work reduces it.

**Known gaps, stated rather than buried:** the whitening gap named here in the first
version of this post is **closed, and closed by an argument** — ROME's normalisation pins
the coefficient at the subject's last token to 1 for every `C`, so no whitened editor could
have hidden an inference effect from these probes (see above; the `C = I` regime is still
demonstrably destructive, ≈8 nat drops on unrelated same-subject facts); the structural
leakage claim is a layer-5 claim until the coefficient sweep is run elsewhere; the edit ran on one layer, one
model, one relation family, and 42 chains rather than the intended 71, because a week of
NDIF outages ate the rest; GPT-2-XL has not been run under the constrained measure; the 41 chains rejected as routing through dissolved states are
plausibly inflated by taking the first `P17` value, which Wikidata does not order by
currency; hand-built sets license an *existence* claim and never a frequency one;
and possession levels move with candidate-set size, so only the ordering is robust.
Added after the gate: they also move with candidate-set *concentration*, which is
not a knob anyone has been setting deliberately, ours included.
