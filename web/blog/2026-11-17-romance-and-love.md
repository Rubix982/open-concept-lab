Love really invokes emotions inside of you, that you never knew you were capable of. It makes you look at another person, and see some future, or desire some future with that person -- a kind of eternal connection of this constant dance of push and pull, of closeness and being near someone who has your presence even when you're not there.

It's like turning a corner and silently wishing you see that person across the hall. Wondering if you go to a place, will you run into them out of pure chance? It's looking at a sunset and wishing they could see this with you. It's somehow hoping their name comes up in conversations naturally, and every time it does, it gives you a sense of warmth that's hard to define but so essential to why we live and what we do.

But do you actually see yourself when it happens? When you land a gaze at the other person and realize what they bring is suddenly <Gloss on="a fit for all the things you have been missing in life so far">Stendhal called part of this <i>cristallisation</i>, in 1822. A bare branch left in the salt mines near Salzburg for a few months comes out covered in shining crystals, the twig underneath no longer recognisable. The mind, he said, does the same to the person it loves: « l’opération de l’esprit, qui tire de tout ce qui se présente la découverte que l’objet aimé a de nouvelles perfections », the work of the mind that draws, from everything it meets, the discovery that the beloved has new perfections (<i>De l’amour</i>, ch. 2).</Gloss>. It's that essence that is impossible to define, but you cannot see life being or acting in any other way. It feels as if you look into a mirror that reminds you of what you are capable of ... it is strange as a man, because now you realize what this is all for, and what it is all worth. You can be a talented, hard working, very dedicated and focused man, but not realize really what future you are capable of building without taking in that WHY for what you are building it for ... or who you are building it for.

<Claude re="a mirror that reminds you of what you are capable of">
Arthur and Elaine Aron built a theory on this in 1986, the self-expansion model (*Love and the Expansion of Self*). Their idea is that people want to grow, and that love is one of the main ways they do it: the other person's perspectives, abilities and world become partly included in your own sense of who you are and what you can do. In 1992 they turned it into a single picture, two circles labelled *self* and *other* that overlap by more or less, and asked people to pick the pair that best describes their relationship (*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 63:596–612).

Your mirror is close to what they describe. The other person becomes part of what you think you are capable of, and part of the why.
</Claude>

<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Pata hi nahi chalta hai ke kab kese hojata hai, aur dil mei akar koi awaaz aur soch beth jati hai, <Gloss on="jese ke do bichre hoi rooh ho jo ke akhir kaar jakar milgai hai aik dosre se">The oldest telling of this I know is in Plato’s <i>Symposium</i>. Aristophanes says people were once whole, were cut in two, and since then each half “is always looking for his other half”; “the desire and pursuit of the whole is called love” (191d, 192e, Jowett’s translation). Rumi starts the Masnavi from a similar longing: “Every one who is left far from his source wishes back the time when he was united with it” (I.4, Nicholson). He means the soul and where it came from, but the shape is the same.</Gloss>. Ke kissi ko unke ankhon mei dekh ker samajh mei aata hai ke zindagi ka asal matlab kiya hai, aur ye sab kerne ke maqsad kiya banna. Ke aik aesa rishta hai jo itna kareeb hai rooh ke, ke iske begair jeena ab jeena nahi lagta hai. Aur na hi zindagi aese guzari ja sekti hai ke jese unko nazar andaaz kerke kahein rakh diya ho.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

You never know when or how it happens. A voice and a thought come and settle in the heart, as if two souls that were parted have at last found each other. Looking into someone's eyes, you understand what life really means, and what all of this was for. A bond so close to the soul that living without it no longer feels like living. Nor can life go on as if they had been overlooked and put away somewhere.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

پتا ہی نہیں چلتا کہ کب کیسے ہو جاتا ہے، اور دل میں آ کر کوئی آواز اور سوچ بیٹھ جاتی ہے، جیسے دو بچھڑی ہوئی روحیں ہوں جو آخرکار جا کر ایک دوسرے سے مل گئی ہیں۔ کہ کسی کو ان کی آنکھوں میں دیکھ کر سمجھ میں آتا ہے کہ زندگی کا اصل مطلب کیا ہے، اور یہ سب کرنے کا مقصد کیا بنا۔ کہ ایک ایسا رشتہ ہے جو روح کے اتنا قریب ہے کہ اس کے بغیر جینا اب جینا نہیں لگتا۔ اور نہ ہی زندگی ایسے گزاری جا سکتی ہے جیسے انہیں نظر انداز کر کے کہیں رکھ دیا ہو۔

</div>
</div>

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Aur chahe ab tak mulaqat hoi, ya na hoi ho, hum sab ke nazar aur zahan mei aik aesi nazar hai jiska hum sab intezaar ker rahe hain -- aik <Gloss on="dedaar"><i>Deedar</i> has no English word. “Sight” or “glimpse” is the nearest, and both miss it: a deedar is a sight of the beloved that counts as a meeting in itself, the thing the waiting is for. Sufi poetry uses the same word for the sight of God.</Gloss> us nazar ka, ke jiska baad zindagi alag badal hi jaeigi. Ke jese pehle kabhi socha nahi tha dil lagane ki kahein kabhi jaga bhi milegi, ke koi aur aik aesa dil mil bhi sekta hai jo ke hamare dil ko samajhne aur "rakhne" ki taqat aur soch rakhta ho.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

And whether or not we have met yet, each of us carries in our eyes and our mind a gaze we are all waiting for, a *deedar* of that gaze, after which life will simply change. As if we had never thought the heart would find a place to settle, or that there could be another heart with the strength and the care to understand ours, and to "keep" it.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

اور چاہے اب تک ملاقات ہوئی ہو یا نہ ہوئی ہو، ہم سب کی نظر اور ذہن میں ایک ایسی نظر ہے جس کا ہم سب انتظار کر رہے ہیں — اس نظر کا ایک دیدار، جس کے بعد زندگی بدل ہی جائے گی۔ کہ جیسے پہلے کبھی سوچا نہیں تھا کہ دل لگانے کی کہیں کبھی جگہ بھی ملے گی، کہ کوئی اور ایسا دل مل بھی سکتا ہے جو ہمارے دل کو سمجھنے اور "رکھنے" کی طاقت اور سوچ رکھتا ہو۔

</div>
</div>

<Claude re="aik aesi nazar hai jiska hum sab intezaar ker rahe hain">
Ghalib wrote the other end of this wait:

<div className="ocl-poem" dir="rtl" lang="ur">
  <p>یہ نہ تھی ہماری قسمت کہ وصالِ یار ہوتا<br />اگر اور جیتے رہتے یہی انتظار ہوتا</p>
</div>

*ye na thi hamari qismat ki visal-e-yar hota / agar aur jite rahte yahi intizar hota*: it was never my fate to be with the one I love; had I lived on, it would only have been more of this waiting.

Your sentence waits for a deedar that will change everything, and his is a wait that never ends. Urdu keeps a word for each part of it: *intezaar* for the waiting, *visaal* for the meeting, *hijr* for the time apart.
</Claude>

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Ajeeb si baat hai -- aam taur per insaan, jese ke mere, ke paas aese bauhat khayaal aate hain, bolne ke, likhne ke, parhne ke -- magar likhte waqt ye sab khayaal aik dam khamoosh hojate hai. Aik aesa parda hai jo insaan ke bol chaal mei rehti hai, aur aik aesi zabaan hai jo insaan ke sirf khayaloon mei rehti hai, chahe kitni gehri soch ho. Wese hi jese ghanto zahan mei khyalat rehte hai ke kiya bolna hai, kese bolna hai, kin alfaazon ka channao kerna hai, kis lehje mei kehna hai, kin soorat-e-haal mei kehna hai -- magar jab insaan iteraaf kerne per aata hai, to insaan ke paas alfaaz hi nahi hote hain.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

It's a strange thing. Ordinary people, like me, have so many thoughts, of things to say, to write, to read, but the moment we sit down to write, they all go quiet at once. There is a veil that lives in the way we speak, and a language that lives only in our thoughts, however deep the thinking. For hours the mind turns over what to say, how to say it, which words to choose, in what tone, in what moment, and then, when it comes to confessing it, there are no words at all.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

عجیب سی بات ہے — عام طور پر انسان کے پاس، جیسے میرے پاس، ایسے بہت سے خیال آتے ہیں، بولنے کے، لکھنے کے، پڑھنے کے — مگر لکھتے وقت یہ سب خیال ایک دم خاموش ہو جاتے ہیں۔ ایک ایسا پردہ ہے جو انسان کی بول چال میں رہتا ہے، اور ایک ایسی زبان ہے جو انسان کے صرف خیالوں میں رہتی ہے، چاہے کتنی ہی گہری سوچ ہو۔ ویسے ہی جیسے گھنٹوں ذہن میں خیالات رہتے ہیں کہ کیا بولنا ہے، کیسے بولنا ہے، کن الفاظ کا چناؤ کرنا ہے، کس لہجے میں کہنا ہے، کس صورتِ حال میں کہنا ہے — مگر جب انسان اعتراف کرنے پر آتا ہے، تو انسان کے پاس الفاظ ہی نہیں ہوتے۔

</div>
</div>

<Claude re="magar jab insaan iteraaf kerne per aata hai, to insaan ke paas alfaaz hi nahi hote hain">
Rumi opens the Masnavi with a reed flute: "Listen to this reed how it complains: it is telling a tale of separations" (I.1–2, Nicholson's 1926 translation). The reed has a voice only because it was cut from the reed-bed where it grew, and everything it plays is about that distance. All six books of the Masnavi begin with that sound.

What you describe runs the other way. The feeling is full, and it is the voice that goes quiet.
</Claude>

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Kissi ko batana, ke jab woh nazar mei ai, to dil ki dharkaan kese barh jaati hai, ke bahana chaiye aik baat kehne ka, unki taraf dekhne ka, unke kareeb aane ka -- magar alfaaz hamesha zabaan per nahi aate hain, aur aesa lagta hai ke humme apne dil ki gehrayon mei pata hai humme kiya chaiye, aur chahne ke liye humme apne aap ko convince kerne ki need nahi parti. Unki tareef hi lajawaab hai, kabhi koi mille to unhe koi hamara ziqar kare, unki muskurahat ke peeche kiya raaz chuppe hai, aur hum dono ke aese mustaqbil ki taraf chal rahe hain jiski poori haqeqat mei dono mei se kissi ko nahi pata -- shayad bas itna hi pata hai ke saath chalna hai, har soorat mei.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

To tell someone how the heart beats faster when they come into view, how you need an excuse to say something, to look their way, to come near them. But the words don't always reach the tongue, and it feels as if, deep in our hearts, we know what we want, and we don't need to convince ourselves to want it. They are beyond any praise. If anyone ever meets them, may they mention us. What secrets hide behind their smile. And the two of us are walking toward a future whose full truth neither of us knows; perhaps all we know is that we have to walk together, whatever happens.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

کسی کو بتانا کہ جب وہ نظر میں آئے تو دل کی دھڑکن کیسے بڑھ جاتی ہے، کہ بہانہ چاہیے ایک بات کہنے کا، ان کی طرف دیکھنے کا، ان کے قریب آنے کا — مگر الفاظ ہمیشہ زبان پر نہیں آتے، اور ایسا لگتا ہے کہ ہمیں اپنے دل کی گہرائیوں میں پتا ہے کہ ہمیں کیا چاہیے، اور چاہنے کے لیے ہمیں خود کو قائل کرنے کی ضرورت نہیں پڑتی۔ ان کی تعریف ہی لاجواب ہے، کبھی کوئی ملے تو ان سے ہمارا ذکر کرے، ان کی مسکراہٹ کے پیچھے کیا راز چھپے ہیں، اور ہم دونوں ایک ایسے مستقبل کی طرف چل رہے ہیں جس کی پوری حقیقت ہم دونوں میں سے کسی کو نہیں پتا — شاید بس اتنا ہی پتا ہے کہ ساتھ چلنا ہے، ہر صورت میں۔

</div>
</div>

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

<Gloss on="Pata hi nahi chalta hai ke dil ko kab kiya hojata hai">Ghalib opens one of his best-known ghazals with the same not-knowing: <i>dil-e-nadan tujhe hua kya hai / aakhir is dard ki dava kya hai</i>. Foolish heart, what has happened to you? What, in the end, is the cure for this pain?</Gloss>, aur kitni raaton ko dil <Gloss on="taranp"><i>Tarap</i> is stronger than “yearning”. It is the word for a fish thrashing out of water, <i>machhli ki tarah tarapna</i>, so it is restless and in the body, with nothing wistful about it.</Gloss> kerta hai aik aese sawaal ke jawaab ki jiska koi saaf jawaab nahi. Mudat hoi guzre, mujhe ye ehsaas hoa ke woh rishte chalte hain jinke koshish ki jaati hai. Jese ke phool ko paani chaiye aur imarat mei rehne walo ke begair imarat ka koi maqsad nahi hoa, aur <Gloss on="darya mei paani agar na bahe to phir gulistan kabhi abaad nahi hosekkega phoolon se">Faiz’s garden waits too: <i>gulon mein rang bhare baad-e-nau-bahaar chale / chale bhi aao ki gulshan ka karobar chale</i>. Let colour fill the flowers, let the new spring’s wind blow; come, so the garden’s work can go on (<i>Nuskha Hai Wafa</i>). In his couplet the garden waits for the beloved to arrive. In yours it waits for the water to reach it, which puts the work on whoever loves.</Gloss>. Wese hi, zindagi hal chal hai, agai barhne ka aur aik dosre ke liye koshish kerne ka naam hai. Agar paani apna maqsad bhool jai aur bas isper naaz kare ke woh to paani hai, magar apne aapko dosron tak na paunchaye, to paani ka asool poora nahi hoga ...

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

You never know what happens to the heart, or when, and on how many nights it aches for the answer to a question that has no clear answer. It took me a long time to realize that the relationships that last are the ones people work at. A flower needs water, a building has no purpose without the people who live in it, and if the water in the river doesn't flow, the garden will never fill with flowers. In the same way, life is movement; it is the name for moving forward and making an effort for each other. If water forgets its purpose and only takes pride in being water, but never carries itself to anyone else, it hasn't done what water is for ...

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

پتا ہی نہیں چلتا کہ دل کو کب کیا ہو جاتا ہے، اور کتنی راتوں کو دل ایک ایسے سوال کے جواب کے لیے تڑپتا ہے جس کا کوئی صاف جواب نہیں۔ مدت ہوئی، مجھے یہ احساس ہوا کہ وہ رشتے چلتے ہیں جن کی کوشش کی جاتی ہے۔ جیسے پھول کو پانی چاہیے، اور عمارت میں رہنے والوں کے بغیر عمارت کا کوئی مقصد نہیں، اور دریا میں پانی اگر نہ بہے تو پھر گلستان کبھی پھولوں سے آباد نہیں ہو سکے گا۔ ویسے ہی زندگی ہلچل ہے، آگے بڑھنے کا اور ایک دوسرے کے لیے کوشش کرنے کا نام ہے۔ اگر پانی اپنا مقصد بھول جائے اور بس اس پر ناز کرے کہ وہ تو پانی ہے، مگر خود کو دوسروں تک نہ پہنچائے، تو پانی کا اصول پورا نہیں ہوگا…

</div>
</div>

<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

... Love is the same. The heart should have the right to speak, too, and it should be allowed to face its fears and its goals.

<Claude re="woh rishte chalte hain jinke koshish ki jaati hai">
Rilke told a young poet much the same in a letter in May 1904: loving another person may be the hardest thing we are ever asked to do, and the rest of our work is a kind of preparation for it (*Letters to a Young Poet*, letter 7).

Half a century later Erich Fromm wrote a whole book on it, *The Art of Loving* (1956). His argument is that love is something you do and keep doing, closer to practising an art than to falling into a feeling. Your water paragraph makes the same point with a river.
</Claude>

<Claude re="Dil ko bolne ka haq bhi hona chaiye">
There's an experiment on this. In 1997 Arthur Aron and four colleagues paired up strangers for 45 minutes. Some pairs took turns answering 36 questions that slowly became more personal; others asked each other small-talk questions. The pairs with the personal questions reported a bigger rise in feeling close (*Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* 23:363–377). The closeness came from saying things, in turns, a little more each time.

One of the early questions asks whether you rehearse what you're going to say before making a phone call, and why. That is your hours of *kya bolna hai, kese bolna hai*.
</Claude>
