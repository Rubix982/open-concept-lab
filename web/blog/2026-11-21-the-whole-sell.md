Over the last few weeks, one thing has slowly become clear to me. I want to write it down before it feels so obvious that I stop noticing it. Building software has become easy.

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Mujhe is poore experience se jo sab se zyada learning mili hai, woh ye hai ke bauhat se areas still aese exist kerte hain jidher aapko customers milenge aapko pay kerne ke liye for basic support on software, bas itni si baat hai -- woh software service based ka zamana ab nahi ke multiple teams ready rahein bas koi bhi customer aane ko -- baat ye hai ke aik single shakhs ke paas power aa gayi hai ke woh aese software scratch se banana shuru kar de -- zaroorat is baat ki hai ke aap need identify kar sako, aur solution propose kar sako -- that is what this age demands now.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

The biggest thing I learned from all of this is simple. There are still many areas where people will pay you for basic help with software. The days of software services, with several teams waiting for any customer to walk in, are over. Now a single person has the power to build that kind of software from scratch. What you need is to spot the need and offer a solution. That is what this age asks for.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

مجھے اس پورے تجربے سے سب سے بڑا سبق یہ ملا کہ بہت سے شعبوں میں آج بھی ایسے لوگ ہیں جو سافٹ ویئر کی بنیادی مدد کے لیے پیسے دیں گے۔ بس اتنی سی بات ہے۔ وہ زمانہ گیا جب کئی ٹیمیں کسی بھی گاہک کے انتظار میں تیار بیٹھی رہتی تھیں۔ اب ایک اکیلا شخص بھی ایسا سافٹ ویئر شروع سے بنا سکتا ہے۔ ضرورت بس یہ ہے کہ آپ ضرورت کو پہچانیں اور اس کا حل پیش کریں۔ یہی اس دور کا تقاضا ہے۔

</div>
</div>

It took me a while to believe it. I grew up thinking software needed a team: people to design it, people to write it, people to test it. It took months before anything worked. Execution was slow and heavy. An idea had to get through meetings and handoffs before anyone could build it. Now one person can have an idea in the morning and have it working by the evening. If it is wrong, they can try again the same night.

But the more I built, the more I noticed where my time really went. Very little went into writing code. Most of it went into finding out what to build, and I couldn't do that alone at a desk. Requirements don't come at the start. Nobody can write them down in advance. Even the person with the problem can't, because they know the problem but not what the answer should look like. The requirements show up when someone looks at the thing and gets lost. We don't know them upfront. We go one by one.

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Aur shayad zindagi bhi isi cheez ka naam hai, hum waqt se pehle nahi bata sekte hain ke final effort kese lagegi, kis soorat ki hogi, kiya masle honge, kiya nuqsan honge, aur kiye begair pata nahi chalega. Zindagi us waqt shuru hoti hai jab hum kam requirements ke basis per chalna shuru kerte hain, aur hum aahista aahista meaning aur goals derive kerna start kerte hain -- insaan ki imagination bauhat kam hai, hum kisi cheez ko shuru kiye begair nahi bata sekte hain ke zindagi kese banegi. What relationships will we find? How will our career progress and grow, how will we find our way? No one knows, but the only thing we can do is keep moving.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

Maybe life works the same way. We can't know ahead of time what the final effort will look like, what shape it will take, or what problems and losses will come. We only find out by doing it. Life starts when we begin walking with only a few requirements. Slowly, we find meaning and goals along the way. Our imagination is small. We can't tell how life will turn out before we start. What relationships will we find? How will our career grow? How will we find our way? No one knows. All we can do is keep moving.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

اور شاید زندگی بھی ایسی ہی ہے۔ ہم پہلے سے نہیں جان سکتے کہ ہماری محنت کا انجام کیسا ہوگا، اس کی شکل کیا ہوگی، کیا مشکلیں آئیں گی، کیا نقصان ہوں گے۔ یہ سب کر کے ہی پتا چلتا ہے۔ زندگی تب شروع ہوتی ہے جب ہم چند تقاضوں کے ساتھ چلنا شروع کرتے ہیں، اور آہستہ آہستہ راستے میں معنی اور مقصد ملتے جاتے ہیں۔ انسان کا تخیل بہت محدود ہے۔ کچھ شروع کیے بغیر ہم نہیں بتا سکتے کہ زندگی کیسی بنے گی۔ ہمیں کون سے رشتے ملیں گے؟ ہمارا کیریئر کیسے آگے بڑھے گا؟ ہم اپنا راستہ کیسے ڈھونڈیں گے؟ کوئی نہیں جانتا۔ ہم بس چلتے رہ سکتے ہیں۔

</div>
</div>

If requirements only show up one by one, then feedback is what you build from. And good feedback is rarer than I thought. Show most people something, and they say it looks good.

All feedback is nice to have, but some of it is worth more than the rest. When someone says "wow, you are a great person", "that painting is beautiful!" or "this music is so serene!", those are compliments. They are not really feedback. You already knew what they told you. And you won't stop painting because one person calls your painting ugly. You have to separate shallow comments from deep, honest criticism.

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Hum insaanon ko vulnerability show kerna aur gherai mei jaane ki bauhat zaroorat hai, ke hum aage barhein us raaste ki taraf jo humein nazar nahi aata hai pehli manzil per, magar dikhna shuru ho jata hai kafi aage -- ye bhi wisdom hai, ke insaan waqt se pehle ye samajhne ki koshish kare ke uski effort usko kis taraf lekar ja rahi hai.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

We need to be open about our weak spots, and we need to go deep. That is how we move toward a road we can't see at the first stop, but which comes into view much further on. It is also wisdom to try to understand early where your effort is taking you.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

ہمیں اپنی کمزوری دکھانے اور گہرائی میں جانے کی بہت ضرورت ہے۔ اسی طرح ہم اُس راستے کی طرف بڑھتے ہیں جو پہلی منزل پر نظر نہیں آتا، مگر کافی آگے جا کر دکھائی دینے لگتا ہے۔ یہ بھی دانائی ہے کہ انسان وقت سے پہلے سمجھنے کی کوشش کرے کہ اُس کی محنت اُسے کہاں لے جا رہی ہے۔

</div>
</div>

Useful feedback sounds different. Someone says they don't understand what a page wants from them, or they doubt it will help with the thing they actually need. My own confusion counts too. More than once, the best requirement came when I used my own work and couldn't tell what had just happened.

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Ke mei itni humility aur shame rakhoon ke koi agar mujhse is waqt, abhi saamne pooche ke ye hai kiya, kerta kiya hai, aur isse hota kiya hai, to mei asaani se bata sakoon -- ke ye cheez kis pareshaani ko door kerti hai, kis khate mei aati hai, aur isse zindagi asaan kese hoti hai. Technology ka usool ye nahi hai ke woh aapko zyada istemaal kerna pare kuch achieve kerne ke liye, technology ka usool ye hai ke kam se kam istemaal ho aur phir bhi kaam achieve ho jaye.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

I want to keep enough humility, and enough shame, that if someone asked me right now what this is, what it does and what comes of it, I could answer easily: what trouble it removes, where it fits, and how it makes life easier. Good technology doesn't make you use it more to get something done. Good technology is used as little as possible, and the work still gets done.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

میں اتنی عاجزی اور شرم رکھنا چاہتا ہوں کہ اگر کوئی ابھی مجھ سے پوچھے کہ یہ کیا ہے، کیا کرتا ہے، اور اس سے کیا ہوتا ہے، تو میں آسانی سے بتا سکوں: یہ کون سی پریشانی دور کرتا ہے، کس زمرے میں آتا ہے، اور زندگی کو کیسے آسان بناتا ہے۔ اچھی ٹیکنالوجی وہ نہیں جسے کام کے لیے زیادہ استعمال کرنا پڑے۔ اچھی ٹیکنالوجی وہ ہے جو کم سے کم استعمال ہو، اور پھر بھی کام ہو جائے۔

</div>
</div>

Then there is finding the ideas in the first place. A need doesn't come from a list of features. It comes from being close enough to someone's work to hear how they describe their week: what they repeat, what they chase, what they still copy by hand. People rarely ask for software. They complain, and the need is usually inside the complaint.

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Insaan se saari dukh dard ki daastaanein sunna zaroori hai, woh kiya sochta hai, ke asal pareshaani woh kiya samajhte hain ke hai kiya, shayad woh apne lehaaz se bataein, aur hum apne lehaaz se ...

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

You have to listen to people's stories of pain and trouble: what they think, and what they believe the real problem is. They may tell it their way, and we may hear it our way ...

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

لوگوں کے دکھ درد کی کہانیاں سننا ضروری ہے: وہ کیا سوچتے ہیں، اور ان کے خیال میں اصل مسئلہ کیا ہے۔ شاید وہ اپنے انداز میں بتائیں، اور ہم اپنے انداز میں سنیں ...

</div>
</div>

And at the end there is the pitch. I used to think of it as a separate skill. But a working tool that nobody uses may as well not exist. Someone has to see it, understand it in a minute, trust it with their work, and decide it is worth paying for. Some of that has nothing to do with the screen. Who is allowed to say yes? What do they need to see before they trust it with their data? Who helps when it breaks? Those are conversations with people. Presentation is the same skill on a smaller scale. A page that clearly says what it wants, from whom, and how, has already been pitched to the person using it.

<div className="ocl-bi ocl-bi--tri">
<div className="ocl-bi__en" lang="ur-Latn">

Presentation bhi hai kiya? Aik validation ke saamne wala jo chah raha tha, wahi hai, ya kuch aur? Humein nahi pata. Bas itna pata hai ke hum agar woh de sakein jo saamne wala tawaqqa kerta hai, to hum succeed kar gaye hain.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__tr" lang="en">

But what is presentation, really? Is it showing the other person that this is what they wanted, or is it something else? We don't know. We only know that if we can give people what they expect, we have succeeded.

</div>
<div className="ocl-bi__ur" dir="rtl" lang="ur">

مگر پریزنٹیشن آخر ہے کیا؟ یہ دکھانا کہ سامنے والا جو چاہتا تھا، یہ وہی ہے، یا کچھ اور؟ ہمیں نہیں پتا۔ بس اتنا پتا ہے کہ اگر ہم لوگوں کو وہ دے سکیں جس کی وہ توقع کرتے ہیں، تو ہم کامیاب ہو گئے۔

</div>
</div>

So this is the step forward as I see it now. Building got cheap. The human part didn't. Feedback, finding ideas, pitching, presenting: that is the whole sell. Human insight matters more than ever. Acting on it matters too, but that is the part that got easier.

What I'm learning is how to understand a customer and their problem, and how to offer something for it. The test from [One Stranger Who Pays](/writing/one-stranger-who-pays) still stands. I pass it only when someone I don't know pays for what I made.
