Lately I've been thinking about problems from a business angle, and more about the people who would pay for an answer than about the answer itself. I want to make something people actually use and pay for. I don't know yet what it is, or how to find it.

A paying customer is the most honest test of whether something is needed. It is the same test I ran on the security ideas this week, where most of them died because someone already sells it or no one would use it. Money is just the strictest version of that test.

What I noticed is that every idea that died started from a product, a feature list or something that sounded clever. The ones that work seem to start from a person who already spends time or money on a problem and handles it badly. So the starting point is not an idea at all. It is someone with a pain.

The first milestone is small: one stranger paying me for something. Not a lot of money, not a company. Small and dull is fine. Dependabot was a small bootstrapped business for a long time before GitHub bought it. I think that one event would teach me more than months of thinking about it.

The filters I used on the security ideas still apply:

- Is it actually needed, by someone I can name?
- Is it too small or awkward for funded companies to bother with?
- Can I keep doing it alone?

There is a tension here I should decide on purpose. I wrote that I spend my time on the layer that is not yet solved, not the layer that already pays. A business is the layer that pays. That doesn't make this wrong. A business can fund the research, or stay a side project. But the two compete for the same weekends, and if I drift into this without deciding, I'll feel guilty about whichever one I'm not doing. It's worth one sitting to say which is the main climb for the next six months.

For reading, [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/) by Rob Fitzpatrick, which is short and is about talking to people without fooling yourself: ask what they did the last time the problem came up, not whether they'd use your idea, because everyone says yes to ideas.

The next step is a list of five people I could actually talk to this month, about a problem they already have. No product in the conversation.
