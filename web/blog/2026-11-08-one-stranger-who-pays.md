Lately I've been thinking about problems from a business angle, and more about the people who would pay for an answer than about the answer itself. I want to make something people actually use and pay for. I don't know yet what it is, or how to find it.

I think, I have come to realization after so many years, that a paying customer is the most honest test of whether something is needed. The value it provides to someone who would be willing to put money in to make it grow is a kind of test that tests the idea in a reciprocal fashion.

It is the same test I ran on the security ideas this week, where most of them died because someone already sells it or no one would use it. Money is just the strictest version of that test. What I noticed is that every idea that died started from a product, a feature list or something that sounded clever. The ones that work seem to start from a person who already spends time or money on a problem and handles it badly. So the starting point is not an idea at all. It is someone with a pain.

The first milestone is small: one stranger paying me for something. Not a lot of money, not a company. Small and dull is fine. Dependabot was a small bootstrapped business for a long time before GitHub bought it. I think that one event would teach me more than months of thinking about it.

The filters I used on the security ideas still apply:

- Is it actually needed, by someone I can name?
- Is it too small or awkward for funded companies to bother with?
- Can I keep doing it alone?

For reading, [The Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/) by Rob Fitzpatrick, which is short and is about talking to people without fooling yourself: ask what they did the last time the problem came up, not whether they'd use your idea, because everyone says yes to ideas.

It would be nice to see areas for those short projects, and how it can become long lasting, something that keeps growing if I spend more time on it.

Time is a war to me sometimes. There never is enough, and it troubles me how fast time moves in two simple glances. We are fighting to build a life for ourself that we don't know how we will reach, how we will get there, what our goal and value is, and what we will leave behind in our wake. 

I have guessed that looking at people who have financial stability in their life -- not having to worry about having a home, a car, or major health problems -- is an utter bliss to live with -- because there isn't a major obstacle towards healthy living as I see it. Money is power, and money is resource, and the split in route for the search of more novel truths and deeper realisations is a different route from the way of producing hard labour for the sake of achieving business goals. We must live with both in mind, as much as we can.

The human nature seeks peace, comfort, but also fire, desire, ambition, and achieving something worth while in the team we exist here today.

So my promise to myself is to take it as seriously as possible, as much as I can, to fulfill my own vows to myself, and use my talents to build something ... you know, others would like to pay for to use.

Perhaps it doesn't need a deeper philsophical dive to get here. It is as simple as narrowing the market, narrowing your offer, making it easy to achieve and get, and sticking to it, you know?
