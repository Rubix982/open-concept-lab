# Writing here

Everything on this page works in any `.md` or `.mdx` file under `blog/` or
`notebook/` with no imports. It doubles as the proof sheet: what you see below
is what the components look like.

## Where a piece goes

|  | Lives in | URL | Ordered by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dated piece — a note, an essay, a session log | `blog/YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md` | `/writing/slug` | Date, newest first |
| Living page — revised in place | `notebook/project/page.md` | `/notebook/project/page` | Sidebar position |

The rule of thumb: if the date is part of what the piece means, it is writing.
If a reader six months from now should see the current version rather than the
original, it belongs in the notebook.

## Journal entries

A dated piece tagged `journal` is set as a notebook page: the day large, the
title in italics, a dot-grid masthead, and a numbered entry (`entry 01`, `02`,
… in the order written). In the `/writing` index it gets a diamond on the
timeline and a ring in the calendar. Nothing else is needed; `standfirst` is
optional. Add `draft: true` to keep an entry off the live site while it's being
written.

## Writing with Claude

For pieces written back and forth: you write, Claude comments, you answer.
Your prose is the page; Claude's words are set in the apparatus face with a
blue rule, so whose words are whose shows without reading a label.

```mdx
A reply between paragraphs:

<Claude re="a more precise question">
The precision may be in the scenario, not the question.
</Claude>

A note on one phrase (keep it on one line):
the <Gloss on="cleaner answer">Cleaner than what?</Gloss> I hoped for.
```

<Claude re="a more precise question">
The precision may be in the scenario, not the question.
</Claude>

A note on one phrase sits in the margin on wide screens: the <Gloss on="cleaner answer">Cleaner than what? The null was clean.</Gloss> I hoped for. On a phone, tap the phrase.

Any post with at least one note gets a **Both / Saif only** switch under its
header, and the `/writing` index marks it "with Claude · n notes".

## Front matter

Dated pieces:

```yaml
---
title: "Watching a claim get smaller"
slug: watching-a-claim-get-smaller
authors: [saif]
tags: [lab-notes, knowledge-editing]
date: 2026-09-10
standfirst: >
  One sentence under the title saying what the piece argues.
status: provisional      # draft | provisional | standing | superseded
revised: 2026-09-24      # only if it differs from date
project: edit-slice
---
```

`standfirst`, `status`, `revised`, and `project` are optional; each one adds a
row to the front matter block at the top of the article. `status` is also drawn
as a stage of growth: draft is a seedling, provisional a plant in bud,
standing an evergreen, superseded a wilted stem. Add `audience:` with a sentence
on who the piece is for, and it shows as an "Assumed audience" card under the
header.

**Illustrations.** Put a drawing at `static/img/covers/<slug>.svg` (or `.webp`,
`.png`, `.jpg`) and the piece gets it automatically: a large picture card on
`/writing`, and the drawing set under the header on the post itself. Set
`cover:` in front matter to use a different path. Pieces without one appear as
compact entries led by their stage of growth. The existing covers are
hand-drawn-style SVGs (an `feTurbulence` wobble over plain strokes, cream
paper, ink, one indigo accent) and are the model to copy. Everything after
`<!-- truncate -->` is hidden from the index.

Notebook pages take `title`, `sidebar_label`, `sidebar_position`, and
`description`. They show a "last edited" line taken from git, so revising a page
is recorded without you maintaining a date by hand.

The notebook sidebar is written by hand in `sidebars.ts`, not autogenerated, so
a new page has to be added there before it appears. The reason is that
autogeneration derives the tree from the files, and the files do not know that a
project with one page and a project with three are the same kind of thing: the
one-page project rendered as a bare directory name and the three-page one as a
tree. Every project is now a category labelled `name · a few words saying what
it is`, and its own overview is listed as a child called **Overview** rather
than being folded into the category.

One trap in that: frontmatter `sidebar_label` wins over the `label` on a
`sidebars.ts` item. A page whose label looks wrong is usually wrong in its own
front matter, not in the sidebar file.

browser shows a broken image, `curl` shows success, and the build reports

## Paper mode

A notebook page can be set as a paper rather than an article: a running head, a
centred title block, justified prose at a smaller size, numbered sections,
figures floated into the column, bordered tables with a shaded header, and
numbered equations. Turn it on by writing the paper's **entry** into the front matter and putting
`<Paper>` at the top of the body with the abstract inside it. Everything that is the
same on every paper — the identifier line, the title block, the author, the keyword
line, the contributions, the figure index — is rendered from that entry. You do not
write any of it twice.

```mdx
---
title: "A cheap pre-edit estimate detects contamination, not propagation"
sidebar_label: Paper
hide_title: true
toc_max_heading_level: 3

paper:
  kind: Working paper
  id: OCL-2026-07
  version: v0.3
  author: Saif Ul Islam
  affiliation: Open Concept Lab
  started: 30 Sep 2026
  updated: 9 Nov 2026
  builds_on: { label: edit-slice, href: /notebook/edit-slice/ }
  reading_guide: >
    Section 7 is the attack surface. If you are deciding whether to spend
    time on this, read the abstract and then section 7.
  keywords: [model editing, ROME, negative result]
  contributions:
    - What this adds.
    - And this.
  index:
    - { label: Figure 1, what: The pinned coefficient (§3.2) }
    - { label: Table 1, what: Coefficient under the stranger control (§5.2) }
---

<Paper>

One paragraph of abstract. Then another.

</Paper>

## 1. Introduction
```

`hide_title: true` is required, because Docusaurus injects its own `h1` when the
markdown has none and a JSX `h1` does not count as one. `toc_max_heading_level: 3`
is worth setting deliberately: a paper's subsections are where a reader navigates —
§5.3, not §5 — and the default stops at `2`.

`id` is this lab's own document number. It is not a preprint identifier and does not
claim to be one.

**Margin notes fall inline on a paper page.** `<Margin>` floats into the gutter on
an ordinary article; a two-column paper has no gutter, so it returns to the inline
form with a left rule. Nothing to do when writing — the note goes where it goes.

**Three pointers carry a paper page, because bold alone does not.** An oxide run-in
through `<Lead>` says *a new move starts here*; an indented paragraph says *this
continues the move above*; a short accent rule above a heading says *a new section
starts here*. Bold inside the body stays ink, so a number or a term does not compete
with a run-in.

**The sections stay as ordinary markdown and are not part of the component.**
Docusaurus builds the contents rail from the markdown syntax tree, so a heading
emitted by a React component never reaches it — the page would render correctly with
an empty rail. `npm run check:papers` enforces the order instead, and it runs on
every build, so a divergent page fails rather than ships.

The mode is applied by a `~` sibling selector from `PaperHead`, so nothing
wraps the body. That matters: wrapping the content in a component hides its
headings from the table of contents.

**Two things never go on a page here.** No email address, and no "cite this
page" or BibTeX block. The byline names a person and Open Concept Lab, which
is ours; a citation block asserts an academic standing that is not held yet.

### The template every paper page follows

The order is fixed, so a reader who has read one project's paper can read the next
without relearning where anything is. Sections are numbered; the front and back matter
are not.

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| | **Front matter** | `<PaperHead>`, then `<Abstract keywords>`, then `<Contributions>` |
| | *Reading guide* | optional `<Aside>` saying which section to read if you are deciding whether to spend time |
| | *Figure and table index* | a two-column table, **required past about eight figures and tables** |
| 1 | Introduction | the problem, why the obvious approach cannot settle it, what this paper finds |
| 2 | Related work | the nearest work, what it does not do, and the novelty position stated plainly |
| 3 | Methods | objects named in English first, then the symbols, then the controls |
| 4 | Implementation | what was built, what it refuses to do, and whether a reading can be taken twice |
| 5 | Results | one subsection per result, in the order they were run |
| 6 | Findings and claims | `<Ledger>` — every claim the page has made, including the withdrawn ones |
| 7 | Limitations | all three categories; see below |
| 8 | Conclusion | what survives, what is open, and what you want a reader to decide |
| | References | `<References ids>` from `data/papers.yml` |
| | Appendix A | the derivations |
| | Appendix B | `<Runs>` — the provenance of every number |
| | Appendix C | `<Readiness>` — what a venue asks for against what exists |

**§7 takes all three categories of limit**, and the third is the one that disappears:
what the result does not show, what was not done, and **how the result may not be
used**. An adversarial heading — *What to attack* — collects the first two well and
loses the third, because nobody attacks a constraint on use. Keep the plain name and
add the entry deliberately.

**§6 is why these are notebook pages rather than papers.** A paper states what its
author believes now. A page revised in place has to keep what they believed before,
with the date and the reason, or a reader who meets a retired claim elsewhere has no
way to know it was retired.

**A section may be empty, and saying so beats deleting it.** A project with nothing
built has an §4 that says so in a sentence. A removed section looks like an oversight;
a one-line section looks like a decision, because it is one.

### The apparatus

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `<Eq gloss="…">` | A display equation. The number is a CSS counter, so equations renumber when you move one. |
| `<Lead>` | A bold run-in at the head of a paragraph — *The task.*, *Scope.*, *Why that cannot bound the damage.* Use these instead of a third heading level. |
| `<Prov id="E-004" />` | A superscript ticket id after a number, linking to the provenance table. Every measured number in the body should carry one. |
| `<Figure wide>` | Lets the figure take the whole column. On a paper page the column is wider than the measure, so this is where the unused space goes; a figure drawn small is capped instead. |
| `<Data caption="…">` | A bordered, numbered table. Put `className="n"` on a cell for right-aligned tabular figures. |

### The record

Three components exist because a notebook page has to say what it believed
before, not only what it believes now.

```mdx
<Ledger caption="Findings ledger.">
  <Finding id="F‑02" status="standing" value="+0.0002"
    falsifier={<><strong>Refuted if</strong> the excess exceeds +0.01 at any layer.</>}>
    In the far field, a stranger's edit reaches a question as much as the real
    subject's edit does.
  </Finding>
</Ledger>
```

`status` is `standing`, `withdrawn`, `superseded` or `denied`; anything but
`standing` strikes the claim through and keeps it on the page. **A `Finding`
with no `falsifier` renders a line saying so**, rather than a blank — the
omission is meant to be visible, because a claim nobody could observe to be
false is not a finding.

`<Revisions>` with `<Rev date version>` rows records what changed and what it
cost. `<Readiness>` with `<Check met need where>` rows is the checklist
against what a venue asks for; it is for the author, not a claim about the
work.

`<Runs>` with `<Run id model n date out>` rows is the provenance table that
`<Prov>` points at. One row per experiment, naming the directory it wrote.

Worked instance: `notebook/edit-reach/paper.md`.

### The PDF

A paper page builds to a two-column PDF with one command.

```bash
python3 scripts/paper_pdf.py notebook/edit-reach/paper.md
```

**The page is the source and there is no LaTeX copy of the paper.** Two copies of
one paper drift, and the drift is invisible until somebody reads both. The script
resolves the page's components into markdown, hands that to pandoc, and wraps the
result in a two-column article class. Citations resolve from `data/papers.yml`, run
markers become superscripts, and each figure is converted from SVG to PDF first,
because LaTeX cannot include an SVG.

It needs `pandoc`, `tectonic` and `rsvg-convert`. The output lands in `pdf/` beside
the page and is not committed: it is a render, rebuilt on demand.

**What it does not reproduce** is the web layout — no contents rail, no hover, no
link into an evidence ledger. Where the page points at something only the site can
give, the PDF says so rather than pretending.

### The overview beside it

A project's overview page is not a short version of its paper. The paper is the
argument, ordered by what rests on what; the overview is the **state of the
work** — what was asked, in what order, what closed, what is still open — which
the paper deliberately leaves out because a reader of an argument does not need
it.

Four components serve that page. `<Glance>` with `<Stat value label note>` is a
row of figures describing the project; big-number tiles earn their place here
because the figures are the point, and they do not belong on the paper where
every number carries a run behind it. `<Cards>` with `<Card id outcome date
denied>` is the card ledger — each question as it was written *before* the run,
with both branches implied by the outcome column. `<Threads>` with `<Thread id
status>` is the thread tree, open and answered together, so the shape of the
inquiry stays visible after the answers arrive.

Worked instance: `notebook/edit-reach/index.md`.

## Claims

The claim block is the one loud element on the site. It shows what was given up
above what still stands, so a narrowing is visible rather than described.

```mdx
<Claim
  superseded="Everyone probes forward from an edit; nobody probes backward."
  record={{
    Confidence: "Medium",
    Revised: "10 September 2026",
  }}
>
  Argument-order inversion is covered. Justification order is not.
</Claim>
```

Use it once per page at most. Its force comes from being rare.

## Margin notes

<Aside>
  On a wide screen this sits in the right margin, beside the paragraph it
  annotates. Below 1400px it folds into the column. Use it for the remark that
  would otherwise become a parenthesis three lines long.
</Aside>

Asides carry the qualification that would derail a sentence. They are not
footnotes — a footnote is for a citation or an aside a reader can skip entirely,
and this site has those too.[^1]

[^1]: Footnotes are plain Markdown: `[^1]` in the text, `[^1]: the note` at the
bottom of the file. They collect themselves into a block at the end.

## Mathematics

Inline math is written `$\mathrm{IIA}$`, which renders as $\mathrm{IIA}$.
Display math takes `$$`:

$$
\Delta_{\text{grounds}}(e) = \frac{1}{|G(e)|}
  \sum_{g \in G(e)} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\, \hat{y}(g) \neq y(g) \,\right]
$$

KaTeX renders at build time, so equations cost nothing at page load and are
selectable as text.

## Citing papers

Papers live once, in `data/papers.yml`, keyed by a citation id:

```yaml
cohen2024ripple:
  title: Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models
  authors: Cohen, R., Biran, E., Yoran, O., Globerson, A., Geva, M.
  year: 2024
  venue: TACL
  url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976
  status: read # read | skimmed | to-read
  relevance: high # to my current work
  verified: true # have I checked the venue against the paper?
  projects: [edit-slice]
  gives_me: The definition of Logical Generalization that narrowed my claim.
```

Cite it inline by id — <Cite id="cohen2024ripple" /> — and it links to the
paper with the full title on hover:

```mdx
The obvious place for it to break is RippleEdits <Cite id="cohen2024ripple" />.
As <Cite id="hase2023localization" narrative /> shows, localisation is not
editability.
```

`narrative` gives the "Hase et al. (2023)" form for when the authors are the
subject of the sentence rather than a parenthetical.

**The `verified` flag is the point of the whole thing.** It records whether the
title, authors, year and venue have been checked against the paper's own
record. An unverified entry renders with a dotted oxide underline, and
`/reading` lists those in a separate table as not citable yet. This exists
because a note of mine described RippleEdits as EMNLP 2023 for months; it is
TACL 2024. Secondhand notes decay silently, and an unmarked wrong venue
survives every draft.

It is deliberately independent of `status`: a citation can be trustworthy for
a paper I have not read, which is the normal case for something on the to-read
list. Everything in the list is currently checked, so that second table is
empty — it reappears the moment a secondhand entry is added.

An id with no entry shows up as <Cite id="nonexistent" /> rather than failing
the build, so a half-written draft still renders.

A reference list at the end of a piece pulls from the same file:

```mdx
<References ids={["cohen2024ripple", "meng2022rome", "meng2023memit"]} />
```

Use `items={[…]}` instead for a one-off that does not belong in the reading
list.

## Interactive pages

A standalone HTML page — plain HTML, CSS and JS, with its own interactions —
embeds as a live page rather than a screenshot. It shares numbering with
`<Figure>`, so a diagram and a demo are both "Figure n".

```mdx
<Embed
  src="https://your-page.netlify.app/"
  title="Belief revision explorer"
  caption="Contraction and expansion applied to the same belief set."
  ratio={16 / 10}
/>
```

Two ways to point at one:

**Hosted elsewhere.** Pass the full URL — a Netlify deploy, a GitHub Pages
site, anything served over HTTPS. Nothing to copy, and the page keeps updating
when you redeploy it. The one requirement is that the host does not send
`X-Frame-Options: DENY`; Netlify does not by default, so this works out of the
box.

**Served from this site.** Add the file to `data/built.yml`:

```yaml
- name: belief-revision
  title: The Belief Problem
  from: mind/belief-revision.html
  topic: Mind
  what: >
    What it takes to give up a belief, and why removing one is harder
    than adding one.
```

`npm run sync:demos` copies it to `static/demos/belief-revision/index.html`,
which happens automatically on `npm start` and `npm run build`. The entry also
becomes a card on [Built](/built) with a live preview. Then embed the path
anywhere you want it in prose:

```mdx
<Embed src="/demos/belief-revision/" title="Belief revision explorer" />
```

Paths in `data/built.yml` are relative to the repository root, and a directory
source is copied whole, so a page with sibling CSS, JS or data files works the
same way. Use `url:` instead of `from:` for a page deployed elsewhere and it
appears on Built the same way, marked with its host. Same-origin is the reason to prefer this route: the page can read
files next to it, and there is no third party to go down.

Either way the embed is sandboxed — scripts and forms run, top-level navigation
does not — and every embed carries a link to open the page on its own, because
an iframe on a phone is a poor place to use an interactive diagram.

Use a fixed `height` instead of `ratio` for a page that does not reflow:

```mdx
<Embed src="/demos/one-crate/" title="One crate" height={620} />
```

## The data files

Three files hold everything that appears on more than one page, so nothing is
described twice:

| File | Feeds |
| --- | --- |
| `data/papers.yml` | `<Cite>`, `<References ids>`, [Reading](/reading), the home page tally |
| `data/built.yml` | `npm run sync:demos`, [Built](/built), the home page list |
| `data/projects.yml` | `<Projects>`, the home page, [Notebook](/notebook) |
| blog front matter | [Writing](/writing), the home page list |

Adding a paper or a page means editing one file. The home page has no
hand-maintained lists.

`projects.yml` is the thinnest of the four — a path to each project's
`plan.md`, a link, and which status dot to use:

```yaml
- name: edit-slice
  plan: ai/edit-slice/plan.md
  notebook: /notebook/edit-slice/
  tone: draft # draft | provisional | standing
```

The words come from the plan itself: the `## Objective` paragraph, trimmed to
whole sentences, and the `## Current Phase` line, shortened to its label with
the rest as hover text. So the way to change what this site says about a
project is to edit that project's plan, which is the file you were going to
update anyway.

```mdx
<Projects />
```

Each project renders as a card with its drawing from
`static/img/art/projects/<name>.svg`, made by `scripts/draw-art.py`.

## Syncing from a project

Notebook pages are ordinary files, so a project under
`~/code/open-concept-lab/ai/<project>/` can keep its own notes and have the page
here written from them. What works in practice is to keep the page shorter than
the notes: the notes are the record, the page is the argument. Copying notes
verbatim produces a page nobody reads, including you.

<References
  items={[
    {
      authors: "Cohen, R., Biran, E., Yoran, O., Globerson, A., Geva, M.",
      year: 2024,
      title:
        "Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models",
      venue: "TACL 12",
      url: "https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976",
    },
    {
      authors: "Meng, K., Bau, D., Andonian, A., Belinkov, Y.",
      year: 2022,
      title: "Locating and Editing Factual Associations in GPT",
      venue: "NeurIPS",
      url: "https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262",
    },
  ]}
/>
