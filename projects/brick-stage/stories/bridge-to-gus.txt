The Bridge to Gus
A short story for brick-figure animation. Six scenes, roughly 4–5 minutes on screen.

Cast
Bolt is a construction worker in an orange safety vest and a yellow hard hat that never sits quite straight. Earnest, overconfident, and permanently frustrated that his C-shaped hands can only hold things that are round.

Maribel is the town baker, in a white apron with a flour smudge printed on her cheek. Calm, quick-witted, and the only one who actually reads instructions.

Old Gus is the lighthouse keeper: white beard piece, blue cap, a slight forward lean from years of looking out at the water. Gentle and a little lonely.

Studs is a small brown dog built from four bricks. He eats anything that comes off a baseplate.

The Townsfolk are a mixed crowd of minifigs: a firefighter, a chef, an astronaut who is clearly from a different set, and a knight who refuses to take off his helmet.

Scene 1 — Morning in Baseplate Town
Setting. A child's bedroom floor, seen at minifig height. The town sits on a bright green baseplate with grey tile roads. Houses are mismatched: a red-roofed bakery, a yellow hardware shop, a blue house with one window built upside down. Morning sunlight pours through a real window far above, laying a warm pale-gold rectangle across the plastic. In the distance, giant and blurry, a sock and a pencil lie like fallen monuments.

Action. Maribel opens the bakery shutters (a satisfying click). On her counter sits the town's proudest creation: a cake made of a single pink 2×2 round brick, a white round tile on top, and one tiny transparent-orange flame piece as a candle.

Bolt bursts in. "Is it ready?"

"It's Gus's birthday cake, not a rocket," Maribel says. "Yes. It's ready."

Bolt reaches for it. His hands close on air above and below the cake. He tries again from the side. Nothing. He tilts his whole body forward, rotates his wrists, and finally grips the candle flame with one hand, lifting the whole cake like a lantern.

Maribel stares. "That's the fire part, Bolt."

"It's the only round part," he says, with total dignity.

Beat: Funny. Establishes Bolt's hands as the running gag.

Scene 2 — The Problem with Rivers
Setting. The edge of town. The green baseplate ends and a river begins: a long strip of glossy translucent-blue tiles running between two baseplates, catching the window light in little white glints. On the far side, on a grey rocky outcrop of slope bricks, stands Gus's lighthouse, red and white stripes, a trans-yellow dome on top. It looks very far away.

Action. The old bridge is gone. Only two lonely grey 1×2 bricks remain, one on each bank, like the bridge simply gave up and left.

Bolt plants his hands on his hips (they don't reach, so he just presses them against his torso). "I'll build a new one. Bolt's Bridge. By lunch."

Maribel looks at the parts pile: three long brown plates, a wheel, a banana, and a single hinge. "With what?"

Studs trots over, sniffs the banana, and eats it with a little clack.

"With less than that, now," Maribel says.

Beat: Funny / setup. The goal is clear: get the cake across the river to Gus.

Scene 3 — Bolt's Bridge
Setting. Midday. The sunlight rectangle has slid across the floor, turning the river a brighter, almost electric blue. The townsfolk gather on the bank, a little crowd of colors: red firefighter, white chef, the astronaut's silver visor, the knight's grey helmet.

Action. Construction montage, fast and bouncy. Bolt places a brown plate. It's crooked. He sits on one end to straighten it; the other end lifts and launches his hard hat into the air. The hat spins, lands on the knight's helmet, and stays there. The knight doesn't notice.

The astronaut offers advice in silent, slow, zero-gravity gestures. Nobody understands.

The chef hands Bolt a wheel. Bolt holds it perfectly; finally, something round. He beams. He then has nowhere to put it and holds it for the rest of the scene.

At last: a bridge. Three plates, joined end to end, wobbling slightly over the blue. The crowd cheers with tiny arm swings.

Bolt takes the cake (by the flame) and steps on. One step. Two steps. The middle plate sags with a soft creak.

Beat: Joyful, then tense. Let the cheer land fully before the creak.

Scene 4 — Into the Blue
Setting. Everything slows. The window light dims as a cloud passes outside; the warm gold drains to a cool grey-blue. The river looks deeper than it did a moment ago.

Action. The bridge folds in the middle, quietly, almost politely. Bolt leaps back to shore. The cake does not.

It tumbles, slowly, end over end, and lands on the blue tiles with a small, final tik. The flame piece pops off and rolls away into a gap between baseplates. Gone.

Silence. Even Studs sits down.

Across the river, a tiny figure appears at the lighthouse window: Gus. He had been waiting. He looks across at the empty bank where everyone was standing a second ago, now scattered and still. He can't see the cake. He only sees that nobody is coming. He slowly turns away from the window. The lighthouse lamp, which had been glowing faintly, goes dark.

Bolt sits on the bank, his hard hat gone, his bald round head shining. He's still holding the wheel.

Maribel sits beside him. She doesn't say anything. After a moment, she rests her hand against his, C-shape against C-shape, and they fit together like a small closed ring.

Beat: Sad. Keep this scene quiet. No music, just ambient room sound: a distant clock, the hum of a house.

Scene 5 — The Minifig Bridge
Setting. Late afternoon. The cloud passes. The light comes back, but different now: deep amber and orange, long shadows stretching from every figure across the green baseplate. The river glows like stained glass.

Action. Bolt looks at the wheel in his hands. Then at his own feet: two small holes on the bottom of each leg. Then at the top of Maribel's head: one stud.

He stands up slowly. "We don't need parts," he says. "We are parts."

Maribel understands immediately. "Everybody! Line up!"

What follows is the big set-piece. The townsfolk click onto each other: feet onto heads, head onto feet, laid sideways and joined end to end, one figure at a time, reaching out over the water. The firefighter. The chef. The astronaut, who for once moves at normal speed. The knight, still wearing Bolt's hard hat on top of his helmet, which makes him exactly one stud too tall and requires a brief, awkward adjustment.

Studs runs back and forth along the bank barking (tiny yip sound effects). Then he runs underneath the growing chain and fishes something out of the river: the cake. Slightly wet. Without its flame. But whole.

The chain reaches the far bank with a final, perfect click.

Maribel takes the cake, and Bolt goes first, walking across his friends' backs. "Sorry. Sorry. Sorry. Sorry," he whispers with each step. The astronaut gives a thumbs-up, which is just his whole hand.

Beat: Great joyful moment. Let the music rise here, building with each click of the chain.

Scene 6 — Lights On
Setting. Dusk. The bedroom is turning purple-blue, the window now a soft rectangle of deep violet. A desk lamp somewhere above has been switched on, throwing a single warm cone of light across the lighthouse. Everything else is cool shadow.

Action. A knock at the lighthouse door. Gus opens it, slowly, expecting nothing.

On the step: Bolt, hatless and soaked from the knees down. Maribel, holding a pink cake with no candle. Behind them, stretching all the way back across the dark river, a glowing line of minifigs, still clicked together, waving.

Gus doesn't speak. He just leans forward a little further than usual.

"It's missing the candle," Maribel says.

Gus looks up at his lighthouse. He climbs the ladder, flicks a switch, and the trans-yellow dome blazes into light, sweeping a golden beam across the river, over the chain of friends, across the whole of Baseplate Town. Every figure lights up, one by one, as the beam passes.

"No it's not," says Gus.

Final gag. Everyone cheers. The knight finally takes off his helmet in celebration, revealing that Bolt's hard hat had snapped onto it, so both come off together. Bolt grabs for the hat, but his hands just close around the knight's round head instead. They freeze. Pause. Studs wanders past in the background, wearing the missing flame piece on his head like a tiny party hat.

Fade out on the lighthouse beam.

Beat: Joyful, warm, a laugh to close.
