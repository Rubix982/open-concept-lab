Edit forensics: reading back facts planted in a model

Someone takes an open model, rewrites one fact with ROME (PoisonGPT demonstrated this in 2023), and republishes it. This page measures how often a defender can name the planted fact from the weights, and whether that survives an attacker who tries to hide the edit. GPT-2 family, CounterFact facts, run locally on one laptop.

Who this is for. The threat is the middlemen between a lab and its users, not the lab's own files. On Hugging Face, Meta's original Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct got 4.88M downloads in the last 30 days, against about 1.26M for the eight biggest third-party copies combined (bartowski's quantized versions alone: 501k). Those few accounts are high-leverage targets, like npm or PyPI maintainers. No in-the-wild case of fact-editing tampering is known to us, so this is pre-emptive work.

Limits

  • GPT-2 only (124M–774M). Published edit-forensics work runs on GPT-J and Llama-3-8B; nothing here shows the numbers hold at that scale.
  • Closed-list subject recovery searches only CounterFact's 20,391 subjects, and the planted subject is always on the list. The open-vocabulary section is the honest version.
  • Object read-back is scored on the first token only, against 34 CounterFact relation prompts. A fact planted under a relation outside that set would be missed.
  • Our ROME is a compact reimplementation, and the MEMIT-style spread is simplified (one output change split evenly across layers), not the published MEMIT.
  • Not tested, and both are needed before the redistributor scenario holds: quantized copies (int8/int4), which is how most third-party copies ship, and facts planted by fine-tuning instead of ROME. A careful attacker would likely use both.
  • The decoy and batch attacks are where the defender weakens; the subspace score that fixes batches was chosen on the batch trials, and its held-out re-run was not completed.
  • Fine-tuning lowers perplexity (27→19) because it trains and evaluates on WikiText; that is not the model getting better in general.
  • The simplified five-layer spread failed to take hold in 7/30 edits; only the 23 that worked are scored.
  • The evasion attacks are the cheap ones. An attacker who knows this exact defender and optimises against it was not tested.
  • Prior work: KSTER (Sun et al., ICML 2026) recovers subjects from the diff given the base and a name list; EditScope (Youssef et al., ICLR 2026) recovers objects without a base. What this page adds is the evasion stress test and a first open-vocabulary trigger search (subject's last word only; the full run was not completed). See notes/prior-art.md.
